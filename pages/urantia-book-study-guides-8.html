Who is the Infinite Spirit?

IAMGODIAM Knowledge Mesh · Urantia Book Study Guides · Paper 8 — The Infinite Spirit · Part I — The Central and Superuniverses · reviewed 2026-10-16

The Infinite Spirit is the Third Person of Deity — the God of Action who springs into existence the very instant the Father and the Son conjointly conceive an identical and infinite action. Paper 8 tells of the Thought-God and the Word-God desiring a universal agent of mutual expression and combined action, and finding him in the Conjoint Actor. With his personalization, the divine personality cycle becomes perfect and complete, and the vast stage of space is set for the drama of creation.

“We are now face to face with the eternity origin of the Infinite Spirit, the Third Person of Deity. The very instant that God the Father and God the Son conjointly conceive an identical and infinite action—the execution of an absolute thought-plan—that very moment, the Infinite Spirit springs full-fledgedly into existence.”

“In thus reciting the order of the origin of the Deities, I do so merely to enable you to think of their relationship. In reality they are all three existent from eternity; they are existential. They are without beginning or ending of days; they are co-ordinate, supreme, ultimate, absolute, and infinite. They are and always have been and ever shall be. And they are three distinctly individualized but eternally associated persons, God the Father, God the Son, and God the Spirit.”

Abba Talk corpus · 8:0.4 · read the passage

What this paper teaches

The paper is careful, first, about time: reciting an order of origin is only a device to help finite minds think about relationships — in reality all three Persons are existent from eternity, without beginning or ending of days. Within that eternity, the Conjoint Creator is wholly and without qualification one with the Father and the Son, and he is the perfect execution of their first completed creative concept. His outstanding attribute is omnipresence, and the paper ends with a warning not to let the Spirit's widespread bestowal and far-flung distribution obscure the fact of his personality: the everywhere-present Spirit is a person, not a force.

Why it matters

Notice what completes the Trinity: not another throne, but an agent of action. The God this paper finishes describing is a God who does — the Conjoint Actor exists to execute, to carry the united concepts of Father and Son out across the whole circle of eternity. And his first recorded act, though he is the full equal of the First and Second Persons, is a voluntary pledge of loyalty: equality expressed as willing service rather than seized status. For communities whose faith has always been a working faith — building, feeding, marching, making — a God of Action is no abstraction. Doing is divine here.
