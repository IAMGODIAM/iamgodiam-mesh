What were the transition years in Jesus' life?

IAMGODIAM Knowledge Mesh · Urantia Book Study Guides · Paper 134 — The Transition Years · Part IV — The Life and Teachings of Jesus · reviewed 2026-10-16

Paper 134 covers the in-between years after the Mediterranean journey. Jesus had studied the peoples and countries he passed through, and he now reached his final decision: he would return to Galilee, to the land of his father Joseph's people, and await the beginning of his lifework as a public teacher of truth — a plan he approved of his own free will. The paper calls these the transition stage of his earth career.

“DURING the Mediterranean journey Jesus had carefully studied the people he met and the countries through which he passed, and at about this time he reached his final decision as to the remainder of his life on earth. He had fully considered and now finally approved the plan which provided that he be born of Jewish parents in Palestine, and he therefore deliberately returned to Galilee to await the beginning of his lifework as a public teacher of truth; he began to lay plans for a public career in the land of his father Joseph’s people, and he did this of his own free will.”

“This was one of the more unusual years in the inner experience of the Son of Man; great progress was made in effecting working harmony between his human mind and the indwelling Adjuster. The Adjuster had been actively engaged in reorganizing the thinking and in rehearsing the mind for the great events which were in the not then distant future. The personality of Jesus was preparing for his great change in attitude toward the world. These were the in-between times, the transition stage of that being who began life as God appearing as man, and who was now making ready to complete his earth career as man appearing as God.”

What this paper teaches

After parting from Gonod and Ganid at Charax, Jesus spent a few weeks at Nazareth with his family — visiting the repair shop with Joseph, talking long with Ruth, and blessing the double wedding of Simon and Jude, after which only Ruth remained home with Mary. He journeyed to the Caspian Sea and rested at Urmia, where his lectures taught that the brotherhood of man is founded on the fatherhood of God, and that freedom, if it is real, belongs to all men alike. Returning, he spent time near Caesarea-Philippi before the season of the feasts.

Why it matters

This paper's center is a sentence many movements could take as law: if one man craves freedom, he must remember that all other men long for the same freedom. Jesus' own freedom in these years is deliberate — he chooses his homeland, his people, and his timing rather than drifting into a public career. The transition is not wasted time; his mind and the indwelling Adjuster are being brought into working harmony. For anyone between a call and its hour, the paper dignifies the waiting: preparation done in obscurity is still obedience.
