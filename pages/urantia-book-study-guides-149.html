How did the second preaching tour of Galilee unfold?

IAMGODIAM Knowledge Mesh · Urantia Book Study Guides · Paper 149 — The Second Preaching Tour · Part IV — The Life and Teachings of Jesus · reviewed 2026-10-16

Paper 149 follows the second public preaching tour of Galilee, October to December of A.D. 28, now carried by Jesus, the twelve, and 117 newly trained evangelists. Jesus himself does little public work; he teaches evening classes while evangelists preach through dozens of towns. His fame as a healer spreads through Palestine and Syria even as Jerusalem's leaders grow alarmed — and the tour closes with everyone recalled to Bethsaida.

“THE second public preaching tour of Galilee began on Sunday, October 3, A.D. 28, and continued for almost three months, ending on December 30. Participating in this effort were Jesus and his twelve apostles, assisted by the newly recruited corps of 117 evangelists and by numerous other interested persons. On this tour they visited Gadara, Ptolemais, Japhia, Dabaritta, Megiddo, Jezreel, Scythopolis, Tarichea, Hippos, Gamala, Bethsaida-Julias, and many other cities and villages.”

“By the time the camp at Bethsaida had been broken up, the fame of Jesus, particularly as a healer, had spread to all parts of Palestine and through all of Syria and the surrounding countries. For weeks after they left Bethsaida, the sick continued to arrive, and when they did not find the Master, on learning from David where he was, they would go in search of him. On this tour Jesus did not deliberately perform any so-called miracles of healing. Nevertheless, scores of afflicted found restoration of health and happiness as a result of the reconstructive power of the intense faith which impelled them to seek for healing.”

What this paper teaches

Before departure, Andrew and Peter asked Jesus to give the final charge to the new evangelists. The tour visited Gadara, Ptolemais, Japhia, Dabaritta, Megiddo, Jezreel, Scythopolis, Tarichea, Hippos, Gamala, Bethsaida-Julias, and many other places. The paper pauses over Jesus' fame as a healer, a series of spontaneous healings it frankly leaves unexplained, his understanding of the minds of men, and the Jerusalem leaders' growing antagonism. Evening classes anchor the account: Simon's group asks why some persons are so much happier and contented than others, and at Gamala Philip asks how the scriptural fear of the Lord squares with looking to the Father without fear. Andrew, with Peter and Jesus' approval, finally dispatched David's messengers to end the tour and recall everyone to Bethsaida.

Why it matters

The founder steps back and the work multiplies: 117 evangelists preach while Jesus teaches at night — leadership measured not by one man's crowds but by the company he trained and trusted. The questions the workers bring back are real questions. Why are some happy and others not? Must God be feared, or can the Father be approached without fear? A faith built for the governed and the afraid answers plainly: fear is not the foundation of sonship. Even the tour's honest admissions serve dignity — the unexplained healings are left unexplained rather than embellished; a movement confident in its message need not manufacture evidence.
