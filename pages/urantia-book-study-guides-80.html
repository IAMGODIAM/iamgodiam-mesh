What this paper teaches

The paper details the westward movement. The violet race's early expansion into Europe was cut short by sudden climatic and geologic changes; the blue man's culture centers stood along Europe's rivers. The Andites poured in steadily, in seven major invasions, the last arriving on horseback in three great waves, while northern Europe was continuously reinforced by migrants through the Turkestan–south Russian regions. As culture declined in the Euphrates valley, civilization's center shifted to the Nile, and a superior culture persisted on the eastern Mediterranean islands. The paper closes by summarizing how Europe's blends generalized into the three white races.

Why it matters

Read against the myth of a self-made West, this paper is quietly subversive: Europe's civilization arrived by migration — teachers, traders, and adventurers carrying Mesopotamian culture into a continent whose own stock had not built a great civilization alone. Even its peoples are blends: Adamic, Sangik, Nodite, and Andonite strains in every 'white race' the paper names. For a Black-first study, the takeaway is the dignity of the carrier and the mixture alike — no continent is owed the story's center, and no people in this history is pure, self-originated, or dispensable to it.
