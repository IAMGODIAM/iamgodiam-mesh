Who was Machiventa Melchizedek?

IAMGODIAM Knowledge Mesh · Urantia Book Study Guides · Paper 93 — Machiventa Melchizedek · Part III — The History of Urantia · reviewed 2026-10-16

Paper 93 tells the story of Machiventa Melchizedek, an emergency Son who took on flesh when revealed truth was threatened with extinction on Urantia. Nearly two thousand years before Jesus, he appeared to a Chaldean herder, proclaimed himself priest of the Most High — the one and only God — and founded the Salem mission. There he taught, formed a covenant with Abraham, trained missionaries for the surrounding peoples, and held the truth of one God until his sudden departure.

“THE Melchizedeks are widely known as emergency Sons, for they engage in an amazing range of activities on the worlds of a local universe. When any extraordinary problem arises, or when something unusual is to be attempted, it is quite often a Melchizedek who accepts the assignment. The ability of the Melchizedek Sons to function in emergencies and on widely divergent levels of the universe, even on the physical level of personality manifestation, is peculiar to their order. Only the Life Carriers share to any degree this metamorphic range of personality function.”

What this paper teaches

The paper first introduces the Melchizedeks as emergency Sons whose range of function is nearly unmatched, and notes the corps of twelve who served as Urantia's receivers after the Adamic default. It then sets the scene — humanity losing ground spiritually after the Adamic miscarriage — before narrating the bestowal: Machiventa's arrival, his first meal with the herder Amdon, and the proclamation of El Elyon under the stars. The Salem chapters follow: schools patterned on the Sethite priests, simple ceremonies of worship, the covenant with Abraham (a chosen individual, the paper insists, not a “chosen people”), the training of missionaries, the end of the mission after Sodom, and Abraham's grief at the disappearance.

Why it matters

Salem's gospel was radically simple: faith and trust in one universal God was the only price of divine favor — no pedigree, no purchase, no priestly toll. And the paper is careful with a word later generations would abuse: it is an error, it says, to speak of “chosen people”; Abraham was a chosen individual, chosen for a task. For communities long told that God's favor belongs to other nations and other names, this ancient correction matters. The Most High who met a herder under the stars does not rank peoples; he calls persons to carry truth to everyone.
