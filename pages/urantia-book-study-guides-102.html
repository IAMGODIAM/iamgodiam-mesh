What does religious faith stand on?

IAMGODIAM Knowledge Mesh · Urantia Book Study Guides · Paper 102 — The Foundations of Religious Faith · Part III — The History of Urantia · reviewed 2026-10-16

Paper 102 answers the materialist who sees man as an evolutionary accident doomed to oblivion. Against that despair it sets saving faith, born in the human heart when moral consciousness sees that human values can be translated from the material to the spiritual, from time to eternity. Faith rests, the paper argues, on the work of the indwelling Thought Adjuster, on religion recognized in the lives of one's fellows, and finally on the evidence of human experience itself.

“But such is not man’s end and eternal destiny; such a vision is but the cry of despair uttered by some wandering soul who has become lost in spiritual darkness, and who bravely struggles on in the face of the mechanistic sophistries of a material philosophy, blinded by the confusion and distortion of a complex learning. And all this doom of darkness and all this destiny of despair are forever dispelled by one brave stretch of faith on the part of the most humble and unlearned of God’s children on earth.”

“The certainties of science proceed entirely from the intellect; the certitudes of religion spring from the very foundations of the entire personality. Science appeals to the understanding of the mind; religion appeals to the loyalty and devotion of the body, mind, and spirit, even to the whole personality.”

What this paper teaches

The paper builds its case in stages. The work of the Thought Adjuster, it says, explains how man's primitive sense of duty is translated into a higher and more certain faith in the eternal realities of revelation. Observing minds know religion when they find it in the lives of their fellows, while intellectual poverty unavoidably handicaps higher attainment by robbing religion of its chief channel of philosophic contact. Knowing the mind of God is no more mysterious, given the Adjuster, than knowing any other mind. The paper concedes that proving belief is not proving what is believed, yet points to the steady fall of false gods before science and reason as faith's ally, and concludes that the highest evidence of religion is the fact of human experience itself.

Why it matters

The passage above says the doom of despair is dispelled by one brave stretch of faith on the part of the most humble and unlearned of God's children on earth. That sentence carries this paper's charge: certainty is not rationed by schooling. The paper is honest that educational poverty handicaps religious attainment — deprivation is real, and it wounds the spirit's chief channels. But the foundation it finally names is experience, which no one can confiscate. For people whose learning was outlawed and whose churches were burned, a faith grounded in lived experience rather than permitted books is not a weakness. It is the surviving evidence.
