Why did Jesus flee through northern Galilee, and what did he teach there?

IAMGODIAM Knowledge Mesh · Urantia Book Study Guides · Paper 155 — Fleeing Through Northern Galilee · Part IV — The Life and Teachings of Jesus · reviewed 2026-10-16

Paper 155 follows Jesus and the twenty-four as they leave the closed doors of Capernaum behind and camp north of Bethsaida-Julias. Planning a tour through Batanea and northern Galilee to the Phoenician coast, Jesus teaches his followers about the rulers who take counsel against the Lord's anointed, warns them against one-sidedness, and at Caesarea-Philippi delivers a memorable discourse on religion — the religion of the spirit — before the tour moves on.

“In all that you do, become not one-sided and overspecialized. The Pharisees who seek our destruction verily think they are doing God’s service. They have become so narrowed by tradition that they are blinded by prejudice and hardened by fear. Consider the Greeks, who have a science without religion, while the Jews have a religion without science. And when men become thus misled into accepting a narrow and confused disintegration of truth, their only hope of salvation is to become truth-co-ordinated—converted.”

“Let me emphatically state this eternal truth: If you, by truth co-ordination, learn to exemplify in your lives this beautiful wholeness of righteousness, your fellow men will then seek after you that they may gain what you have so acquired. The measure wherewith truth seekers are drawn to you represents the measure of your truth endowment, your righteousness. What this paper teaches

The paper opens in a beautiful park south of Bethsaida-Julias, where Jesus calls his followers around him and discusses the plans for the northern tour. He applies the Psalmist's words about the kings of the earth and the rulers taking counsel together against the Lord and his anointed, and contrasts the Greeks, who have a science without religion, with the Jews, who have a religion without science. Peter is sent over to Chorazin with the twelve evangelists while Jesus, with the eleven, departs for Caesarea-Philippi, where they tarry and teach for two weeks. On Thursday morning, June 9, the company leaves Caesarea-Philippi, and Jesus gives the memorable discourse on religion, pausing in the shade to continue teaching the religion of the spirit.

Why it matters

This is a paper about wholeness under pressure. Jesus names the defect on both sides: a people can hold science without religion, or religion without science, and both narrowings blind. The Pharisees who seek his destruction, he says, verily think they are doing God's service, so narrowed by tradition that they are blinded by prejudice and hardened by fear. Against all that stands the truth-co-ordinated life he describes: people so whole that truth seekers are drawn to them, needing no frantic recruitment. For communities told to choose between faith and learning, body and spirit, survival and conviction, this hillside teaching refuses the choice — salvation lies in becoming truth-co-ordinated, converted, whole.
