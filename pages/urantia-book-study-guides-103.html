Is religious experience real?

IAMGODIAM Knowledge Mesh · Urantia Book Study Guides · Paper 103 — The Reality of Religious Experience · Part III — The History of Urantia · reviewed 2026-10-16

Yes — and the paper explains how that experience is built into human beings. All truly religious reactions, it says, are sponsored by the adjutant of worship and censored by the adjutant of wisdom, and the Thought Adjuster becomes the cosmic window through which finite creatures glimpse the certainties of Deity. Religious experience is therefore not an accident of temperament: it has sponsors in the mind, a history in evolution, and fruits by which it can be recognized.

“While your religion is a matter of personal experience, it is most important that you should be exposed to the knowledge of a vast number of other religious experiences (the diverse interpretations of other and diverse mortals) to the end that you may prevent your religious life from becoming egocentric—circumscribed, selfish, and unsocial.”

What this paper teaches

The paper traces religious experience from its roots upward. Its unity across a social or racial group derives from the identical nature of the God fragment indwelling each individual, and religion is functional in the human mind before it ever appears in consciousness. The clan and tribal spirit of solidarity helped shape it; the mark of a religious gathering, unlike a social occasion, is its atmosphere of communion. Early minds derived social duty and moral obligation chiefly from emotional fear. Theology, the study of the actions and reactions of the human spirit, can never become a science, for science is sustained by reason while religion is sustained by faith. Science and philosophy may argue God's probability; only the personal experience of a spirit-led man affirms his certainty.

Why it matters

This paper will not let faith stay private property. Religious unity, it says, grows from the same divine fragment in every person, and its own counsel insists that believers expose themselves to many other religious experiences lest their religion become egocentric — circumscribed, selfish, and unsocial. That is a word for communities that survived by testifying together: the communion that fills a true religious gathering is not decoration, it is the evidence. A faith that cannot sit at the table with other people's experience of God has, by this paper's measure, mistaken itself. Real religious experience makes neighbors, not enclaves.
