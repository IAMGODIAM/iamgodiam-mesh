What this paper teaches

The paper describes the watchers—Gabriel and the celestial hosts under the Personalized Adjuster—waiting in anxious expectancy as they saw the mortal body in the tomb and detected evidences of universe activity. It records what the resurrection was not: the physical body remained undisturbed in the sepulchre, the stones unmoved, Pilate's seals unbroken, and Jesus emerged neither as a mere spirit nor in his pre-incarnation form as Creator Sovereign. It then follows Gabriel inaugurating the general resurrection of the Adamic dispensation, the ten apostles asleep at the Mark home, and Peter racing toward the tomb, his thoughts alternating between fear and hope.

Why it matters

No human committee engineers this resurrection, and no empire authorizes it. The most powerful beings present can only watch and wait; the life is taken up again by the one who laid it down. For people whose movements have been declared dead by every authority in the room, that is the paper's thunder: the verdict of the tomb is not the last word, and it is not negotiated. Note, too, where faith begins—not in triumph but in Peter running through the dark, afraid to meet the Master and hoping anyway. Resurrection faith is born among the frightened, not the finished.
