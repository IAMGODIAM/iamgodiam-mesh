What was Jesus' early childhood like?

IAMGODIAM Knowledge Mesh · Urantia Book Study Guides · Paper 123 — The Early Childhood of Jesus · Part IV — The Life and Teachings of Jesus · reviewed 2026-10-16

Paper 123 follows the holy family out of danger into ordinary life. In Alexandria they settled with kinsfolk while Joseph worked as a carpenter and rose to foreman over a large crew on a public building. The family sailed for Joppa and spent September in Bethlehem deciding where to settle, then returned to Nazareth, where Jesus — about three years and two months old — arrived in excellent health, missing his Alexandrian playmates but delighting in a home of his own.

“Joseph and Mary finally took leave of Alexandria on a boat belonging to their friend Ezraeon, bound for Joppa, arriving at that port late in August of the year 4 B.C. They went directly to Bethlehem, where they spent the entire month of September in counsel with their friends and relatives concerning whether they should remain there or return to Nazareth.”

What this paper teaches

The paper traces the child's first years step by step. It tells of the family's life in Alexandria and Joseph's new experience, which planted the idea of becoming a contractor and builder after the return. It recounts the journey home and the settlement in Nazareth. It marks the boy's first personal and wholehearted moral decision, when a Thought Adjuster came to abide with him. It describes his language learning — the Galilean dialect of Aramaic mastered with his mother's help, then Greek begun under his father's teaching. It follows an eventful year, his seventh year, when formal education in the synagogue schools began, and an interesting year at school.

Why it matters

This is a refugee childhood, told without embarrassment. The family fled a violent ruler, lived on kinfolk's hospitality in a foreign city, and rebuilt a livelihood by the father's hands. Jesus grew up inside a kept secret: Joseph persuaded Mary that it would be unwise to spread the word that Jesus was a child of promise, and both parents faithfully told no one. For children raised amid displacement, working parents, and unspoken burdens, this paper gives a Savior who knew exile and ordinary school days — holiness growing up in a laboring household, not apart from it.
