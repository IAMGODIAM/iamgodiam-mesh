How does the state come into being?

IAMGODIAM Knowledge Mesh · Urantia Book Study Guides · Paper 71 — Development of the State · Part III — The History of Urantia · reviewed 2026-10-16

The state, Paper 71 teaches, is not a divine invention or a deliberate design. It is an evolutionary institution — society's net gain from the ravages and sufferings of war, and statecraft is simply the accumulated technique for adjusting the contest of force between tribes and nations. The modern state survived the long struggle for group power; superior force prevailed and produced it. The paper then follows the state from primitive beginnings toward the enduring form: a government founded on culture, dominated by ideals, and motivated by service.

“The modern state is the institution which survived in the long struggle for group power. Superior power eventually prevailed, and it produced a creature of fact—the state—together with the moral myth of the absolute obligation of the citizen to live and die for the state. But the state is not of divine genesis; it was not even produced by volitionally intelligent human action; it is purely an evolutionary institution and was wholly automatic in origin.”

What this paper teaches

The paper defines the state as a territorial social regulative organization, strongest and most enduring when it is a single nation sharing a common language, mores, and institutions. It treats democracy as an ideal that is a product of civilization, not of evolution, and argues that a government's form matters little provided it affords liberty, security, education, and social co-ordination. Economics, society, and government must evolve if they are to remain; competition is essential to progress but breeds violence unregulated; and profit motives must be augmented by service motives. The one sacred feature of any government is the division of statehood into executive, legislative, and judicial functions.

Why it matters

For a people whose governments were so often built by conquest and used against them, this paper's demystification is power: the state is not of divine genesis, so no ruler's order is heaven's order, and what evolved can be re-evolved toward justice. The tests the paper sets are not complicated machinery but essentials owed to every person — liberty, security, education, and social co-ordination. Where those are denied, government fails its own definition. And the paper's economic warning cuts deep: a profit-only economy is doomed unless profit is augmented by service, which names the daily work of mutual aid and institution-building as the state's true foundation.
