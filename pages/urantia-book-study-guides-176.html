What did Jesus tell his apostles about his return?

IAMGODIAM Knowledge Mesh · Urantia Book Study Guides · Paper 176 — Tuesday Evening on Mount Olivet · Part IV — The Life and Teachings of Jesus · reviewed 2026-10-16

Leaving the temple on Tuesday afternoon, Matthew admired its massive stones, and Jesus answered that not one stone would be left upon another. That evening on Mount Olivet the apostles pressed him with questions. He warned them that Jerusalem would be destroyed and told his followers how to escape its fall; he spoke of his promised return, whose time no one knows; and when Thomas asked what their attitude should be while he was away on the Father's business, he taught them how to wait and work.

“The apostles sat in silence in the moonlight for a considerable time while these astounding predictions of the Master sank into their bewildered minds. And it was in conformity with this very warning that practically the entire group of believers and disciples fled from Jerusalem upon the first appearance of the Roman troops, finding a safe shelter in Pella to the north.”

What this paper teaches

The paper is really three conversations. First, the warning: answering Nathaniel, Jesus described the city's coming overthrow and instructed his followers to flee — instructions so well remembered that practically the whole group of believers escaped to Pella when the Roman troops first appeared. Second, the promise: Jesus said he would return with power and in the spirit, and that the times of his reappearing are known only in the councils of Paradise. Third, the correction: throughout the evening he took particular pains to keep the apostles from fusing the destruction of Jerusalem with his second coming — a fusion they made anyway, and kept making.

Why it matters

Jesus' prophecy in this paper is pastoral before it is spectacular: he gives the warning because he does not want his teachers and preachers to perish in the overthrow. The same care governs what he refuses to give — no date, no timetable, nothing to build a frenzy on. When believers under pressure hunger for the end to come quickly, this paper answers with work: proclaim the gospel to all the world, and let the fullness of the age belong to the Father. Readiness here is faithfulness over time, not alarm.
