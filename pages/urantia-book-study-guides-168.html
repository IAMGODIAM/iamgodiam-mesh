What happened at the resurrection of Lazarus?

Paper 168 tells the raising of Lazarus. He has been dead four days, laid in the family's tomb at the end of the garden, when Martha — who has kept a lad watching the Jericho road — runs to meet Jesus near Bethany. She falls at his feet with no word of doubt: if he had been here, her brother would not have died. Jesus comforts Martha and Mary, asks where they have laid him, and goes with them to the tomb.

“IT WAS shortly after noon when Martha started out to meet Jesus as he came over the brow of the hill near Bethany. Her brother, Lazarus, had been dead four days and had been laid away in their private tomb at the far end of the garden late on Sunday afternoon. The stone at the entrance of the tomb had been rolled in place on the morning of this day, Thursday.”

What this paper teaches

The paper fills in the household: Lazarus and his sisters, children of a leading resident of Bethany, respected followers of Jesus who had inherited vineyards and olive orchards, both parents already resting in the family tomb. It recalls the puzzling word the messenger carried back — 'this sickness is really not to the death' — and Martha's confession: she has long believed Jesus is the Deliverer, the Son of the living God. At the tomb forty-five mortals watch as Lazarus, wrapped in linen bandages, comes forth. The testimony consolidates the faith of believers, though it does little to change the religious leaders' attitude. On the road back to Pella the apostles ask many questions, all answered except those about the details of the resurrection of the dead.

Why it matters

Before there is any miracle, there is Martha's faith: she runs to meet Jesus with the stone already sealed, speaks her grief plainly — if you had been here — and confesses him the Son of the living God while her brother is still in the tomb. The sign, when it comes, happens not in a temple but at a family's garden grave, among mourners and neighbors. And the paper is honest about the aftermath: the same testimony that steadies believers leaves the powerful unmoved. Evidence alone does not liberate; but for the sisters of Bethany, and for every household that has kept watch by a road hoping for help to come, death does not speak the last word.
