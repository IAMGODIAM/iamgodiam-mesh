What did Jesus do on his tour of the Roman world?

IAMGODIAM Knowledge Mesh · Urantia Book Study Guides · Paper 130 — On the Way to Rome · Part IV — The Life and Teachings of Jesus · reviewed 2026-10-16

Paper 130 narrates the tour of the Roman world that consumed most of Jesus' twenty-eighth year and all of his twenty-ninth. He left Jerusalem in April of A.D. 22 with two natives of India — Gonod and his son Ganid — and parted from them at Charax on the Persian Gulf in December of the following year. Along the way he taught the son, interpreted for the father's business, and made the close personal contacts with ordinary people that marked all his pre-ministry years.

“THE tour of the Roman world consumed most of the twenty-eighth and the entire twenty-ninth year of Jesus’ life on earth. Jesus and the two natives from India—Gonod and his son Ganid—left Jerusalem on a Sunday morning, April 26, A.D. 22. They made their journey according to schedule, and Jesus said good-bye to the father and son in the city of Charax on the Persian Gulf on the tenth day of December the following year, A.D. 23.”

“On this Mediterranean tour Jesus spent about half of each day teaching Ganid and acting as interpreter during Gonod’s business conferences and social contacts. The remainder of each day, which was at his disposal, he devoted to making those close personal contacts with his fellow men, those intimate associations with the mortals of the realm, which so characterized his activities during these years that just preceded his public ministry.”

What this paper teaches

The paper follows the journey port by port. At Joppa Jesus met Gadiah, a Philistine interpreter who worked for Simon a tanner. At Caesarea a damaged steering paddle delayed the sailing, and the visit proved eventful before the party departed for Alexandria in Egypt. There, the night before leaving, Ganid and Jesus had a long visit with a government professor who lectured on Plato. In Crete the travelers had but one purpose: to play, walk the island, and climb the mountains, where Jesus held a long talk with a fearful and downcast young man. En route to Carthage the talk ran to things social, political, and commercial, and the first stop on the way to Italy was Malta.

Why it matters

The Son of God prepared for ministry as a tutor and interpreter in another family's employ — and as a student. From Gonod and his brilliant son he learned much about the civilization and culture of India and China, even as he taught them. He rested in Crete without apology and stopped in the mountains for one discouraged young man. For workers, translators, and teachers whose labor goes mostly unnoticed, this paper shows world-changing preparation hidden inside daily faithfulness — and a gospel wide enough to learn from every people it meets along the road.
