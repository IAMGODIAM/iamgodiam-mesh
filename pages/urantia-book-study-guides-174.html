What did Jesus teach in the temple on Tuesday morning?

IAMGODIAM Knowledge Mesh · Urantia Book Study Guides · Paper 174 — Tuesday Morning in the Temple · Part IV — The Life and Teachings of Jesus · reviewed 2026-10-16

Tuesday began at seven in the morning at Simon's house in Bethany, where Jesus said farewell to Lazarus, to the aged Simon, and to the women's corps, whom he never again formally addressed. Then, walking down toward Jerusalem with Andrew, Peter, James, and John, he settled a dispute between Peter and James about the forgiveness of sin. In the temple the questioning resumed — Sadducees with their entangling questions, and at noon a delegation of believing Greeks seeking him out.

“After a short silence Jesus looked significantly at all four and answered: “My brethren, you err in your opinions because you do not comprehend the nature of those intimate and loving relations between the creature and the Creator, between man and God. You fail to grasp that understanding sympathy which the wise parent entertains for his immature and sometimes erring child. It is indeed doubtful whether intelligent and affectionate parents are ever called upon to forgive an average and normal child. Understanding relationships associated with attitudes of love effectively prevent all those estrangements which later necessitate the readjustment of repentance by the child with forgiveness by the parent.”

“Your inability or unwillingness to forgive your fellows is the measure of your immaturity, your failure to attain adult sympathy, understanding, and love. You hold grudges and nurse vengefulness in direct proportion to your ignorance of the inner nature and true longings of your children and your fellow beings. Love is the outworking of the divine and inner urge of life. It is founded on understanding, nurtured by unselfish service, and perfected in wisdom.”

What this paper teaches

The paper braids partings with public debate. Jesus instructed Lazarus in a way that soon sent him fleeing to Philadelphia in Perea and gave his parting advice to the women's corps, while the other apostles established the Gethsemane camp. The forgiveness teaching answers Peter and James: intelligent, affectionate parents are rarely called on to forgive a normal child, because understanding sympathy prevents the estrangement in the first place. In the temple, a council of the Sanhedrin and fifty leaders plotted on Monday night; the Sadducees' resurrection questions were answered until they dared ask no more, and a second group sent to entangle him about angels gave up.

Why it matters

Forgiveness here is not a transaction but a relationship so understanding that estrangement never takes root. What blocks it, Jesus says, is ignorance of “the inner nature and true longings” of our children and fellow beings — we hold grudges in proportion to what we refuse to know about one another. For communities ordered to forgive cheaply, this teaching sets a higher bar: first understand, deeply, and let love do the preventing. Grudge-nursing is named a failure of maturity, not a form of strength. Understanding, in this paper, is the daily labor that makes pardon almost unnecessary.
