How did Jesus carry his family through his adolescent years?

IAMGODIAM Knowledge Mesh · Urantia Book Study Guides · Paper 127 — The Adolescent Years · Part IV — The Life and Teachings of Jesus · reviewed 2026-10-16

Paper 127 finds Jesus in his adolescent years as head and sole support of a large family. Within a few years after his father's death, all their property was gone except the home and garden. He carried the household through agitation over Roman taxes and the slow sale of everything the family owned, while growing relationships at home steadied — and while he became increasingly conscious that he was on earth to reveal his Paradise Father to the children of men.

“AS JESUS entered upon his adolescent years, he found himself the head and sole support of a large family. Within a few years after his father’s death all their property was gone. As time passed, he became increasingly conscious of his pre-existence; at the same time he began more fully to realize that he was present on earth and in the flesh for the express purpose of revealing his Paradise Father to the children of men.”

What this paper teaches

The paper traces how the young provider was formed. It reviews his preparation through infancy and an uneventful childhood. It describes the considerable agitation, especially at Jerusalem and in Judea, in favor of rebellion against paying taxes to Rome, and Jesus living through that pressure. It records the course of the year when all the family property except the home and garden was disposed of. It notes that Jesus and Mary were getting along much better by this time, and that although Jesus was poor, his social standing in Nazareth was in no way impaired. It closes with the story of Rebecca's love for Jesus, whispered about Nazareth and later Capernaum.

Why it matters

Adolescence under economic freefall is holy ground in this paper. Jesus tasted the actual experience of living these years on a world beset by evil and distraught by sin, and so became forever the understanding refuge for distressed and perplexed adolescents of all ages and on all worlds. For teenagers holding families together — working, grieving, refusing both easy rebellion and easy escape — this is not a Lord who watched their struggle from a safe distance. He earned his sovereignty inside it, year by year, and he remains a refuge who understands because he lived it.
