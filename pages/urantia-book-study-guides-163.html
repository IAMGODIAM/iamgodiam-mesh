Who were the seventy Jesus ordained at Magadan?

IAMGODIAM Knowledge Mesh · Urantia Book Study Guides · Paper 163 — Ordination of the Seventy at Magadan · Part IV — The Life and Teachings of Jesus · reviewed 2026-10-16

Paper 163 tells how the seventy were chosen and sent. After the return from Jerusalem, Abner and some fifty disciples arrive from Bethlehem, joining the evangelistic corps, the women's corps, and about one hundred fifty tried disciples at Magadan Camp. For two weeks Jesus and the twelve give this company intensive training. Then, on Sabbath afternoon, November 19, Jesus ordains seventy of them as gospel preachers and teachers, places Abner at their head, and sends them out by couples into all the cities of Galilee, Samaria, and Judea.

“Jesus gave a talk to this company each morning. Peter taught methods of public preaching; Nathaniel instructed them in the art of teaching; Thomas explained how to answer questions; while Matthew directed the organization of their group finances. The other apostles also participated in this training in accordance with their special experience and natural talents.”

What this paper teaches

The training was practical and shared among the apostles: Jesus addressed the company each morning, Peter taught methods of public preaching, Nathaniel instructed them in the art of teaching, Thomas explained how to answer questions, and Matthew directed the group's finances. The seventy themselves were Abner and ten of the former apostles of John, fifty-one of the earlier evangelists, and eight other disciples who had distinguished themselves in kingdom service. A committee of Andrew, Abner, and the acting head of the evangelistic corps turned away more than fifty who sought ordination, though Jesus himself never rejected anyone who craved it. The paper also records Jesus' talk with Matadormus, the rich young man, and the seventy's return to the new camp near Pella.

Why it matters

The kingdom advances here by multiplication, not by monopoly. Jesus does not hoard the work: he trains ordinary, tested people — evangelists, the women's corps, John's former apostles — and trusts them in pairs with real responsibility. The preparation comes before the appointment: two weeks of teaching in preaching, teaching, answering, and even finances. And the gate is honest without being cruel — a committee could say no, but the Master himself turned no seeker away. Communities building durable institutions can recognize the pattern: dignity is honored when people are equipped first and sent with trust.
