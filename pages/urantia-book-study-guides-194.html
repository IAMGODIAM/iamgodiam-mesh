What this paper teaches

The paper defines the message at stake. The gospel of the kingdom is the fatherhood of God joined to the sonship and brotherhood of men; Christianity, as it developed from that day, centers God as the Father of the Lord Jesus Christ and the believer’s fellowship with the risen Christ. The believers feel translated into a new existence of joy, power, and glory, and some think they are discerning what Jesus meant by the kingdom coming with power. Later sections recall the gospel that redeemed man from the superstition of being a child of the devil, note the strange teachings that gathered around early Pentecost stories, and remember how completely demoralized the apostles had been after the crucifixion.

Why it matters

The Spirit in this paper does not descend on the secure; it visits people who have hidden for forty days and turns prayer into speech. Its first gifts are not status but joy, security, and confidence, and its proof is that frightened believers go outside. The content matters as much as the courage: no one is a child of the devil, because the Father’s household makes sons and daughters, and brotherhood follows from that dignity. For communities taught to whisper about their own worth, Pentecost here is the moment heaven authorizes a public voice.
