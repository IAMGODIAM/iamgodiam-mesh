What this paper teaches

The paper works through Deity's several natures and then its hardest questions. Ubiquity — not a confused omnipresence — is what unifies time-space manifestations for finite minds. Many theological dilemmas, it argues, come from mislocating Deity personality and assigning absolute attributes to subordinate Divinity. Omnipotence does not imply the power to do the nondoable, and God, though omnipotent, is not omnificent — he does not personally do all that is done. Creator will and creature will operate within limits set by the Master Architects, and free will is hedged about with restraints. The paper ends by defining providence against fatalism.

Why it matters

Providence does not mean that God has decided all things for us in advance. For people whose suffering has been preached back to them as heaven's fixed script, that sentence is oxygen. This paper leaves the future genuinely open: wills are real, limits are real, and what creatures choose inside those limits matters to what the universe becomes. Faith, then, is not submission to a finished script but participation in an unfinished one — and no preacher, ruler, or system gets to call its own decrees the will of God.
