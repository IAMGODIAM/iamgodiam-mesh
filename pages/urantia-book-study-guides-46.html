What is Jerusem, our system headquarters, like?

IAMGODIAM Knowledge Mesh · Urantia Book Study Guides · Paper 46 — The Local System Headquarters · Part II — The Local Universe · reviewed 2026-10-16

Jerusem, the headquarters of Satania, is an average capital of a local system — typical of its kind despite the irregularities occasioned by the Lucifer rebellion and the bestowal of Michael on Urantia. The paper says our system has passed through stormy experiences but is now administered most efficiently, with order and good will being restored. What follows is a tour: a sphere of a thousand sectors, seven major capitals, superbly controlled energy, gentle all-pervading light, and administrative precincts overseeing the affairs of 619 inhabited worlds.

“Jerusem is divided into one thousand latitudinal sectors and ten thousand longitudinal zones. The sphere has seven major capitals and seventy minor administrative centers. The seven sectional capitals are concerned with diverse activities, and the System Sovereign is present in each at least once a year.”

What this paper teaches

The tour begins with time: a standard mile of about seven Urantia miles, the gradant weight built on the ultimaton, a Satania day of three Urantia days, less an hour, and a system year of one hundred Jerusem days broadcast by the master chronoldeks. Jerusem's energy circulates in zone channels fed from the charges of space, holding full-light temperature near 70 degrees; its lighting sifts evenly out of the sky with no days, nights, or seasons. There are no earthquakes or rainfalls, but beauteous highlands. Broadcasts from the superuniverse and Paradise-Havona arrive in liaison with Salvington through the polar crystal, the sea of glass. Residential circles house the major groups of universe life, a thousand departmental squares carry on executive work, and the hundred triangles direct routine affairs.

Why it matters

Call it the paper's quiet promise: after rebellion and storm, a capital can be brought back — slowly but surely — to order and good will. Restoration here is not a slogan; it is administration, maintenance, and time. Communities rebuilding after catastrophe are often told to expect either miracles or nothing; Jerusem shows a third way, the steady eradication of disharmony's results, age by age. There is comfort in the details, too: a headquarters with mild light, even temperature, and room reserved for every order of life. Heaven, described this plainly, looks like a city where everybody's group has an assigned place to live.
