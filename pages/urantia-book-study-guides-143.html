What did Jesus teach on the road through Samaria?

IAMGODIAM Knowledge Mesh · Urantia Book Study Guides · Paper 143 — Going Through Samaria · Part IV — The Life and Teachings of Jesus · reviewed 2026-10-16

Paper 143 follows Jesus and the twelve as they leave Jerusalem under growing pressure and travel north through Samaria — territory their people had despised for six hundred years. They preach at Bethel, teach Jews and Samaritans together for more than two weeks, and hold their first gatherings among gentiles in the Greek cities of Archelais and Phasaelis. Along the way Jesus teaches the difference between John's self-denial and his own way of self-forgetfulness and self-control, and at Jacob's well he talks with Nalda of Sychar.

What this paper teaches

Driven from Jerusalem by the rulers' opposition, the party stored its gear at Lazarus's home in Bethany and preached through Bethel and the towns of southern Samaria, where the people heard Jesus gladly. In August they made headquarters at Archelais and Phasaelis, their first experience preaching to well-nigh exclusive gatherings of gentiles, and an impassioned address there gave the apostles' message a new note of courageous dominance. The paper portrays the Master's perfected self-control — reviled, he reviled not — and contrasts John's self-denial with Jesus' self-forgetfulness. It recounts the six-hundred-year enmity between Jews and Samaritans, the conversation with Nalda at Jacob's well, the harvest evening at Sychar, and the conferences on Mount Gerizim.

Why it matters

Six hundred years of contempt said Samaritans were beyond the circle; Jesus preached there for weeks and let a Samaritan woman draw out a whole town to meet him. The crossing matters: dignity is not waiting for the permission of those who inherited the prejudice. There is also a discipline for the oppressed in this paper. The Master's self-control — answering reviling without returning it, suffering without threats — is not weakness but governed strength, a refusal to let tormentors set the terms of the soul. And the move from self-denial to self-forgetfulness frees faith from self-punishment: the goal is not a smaller self, but a self mastered and given away in service.
