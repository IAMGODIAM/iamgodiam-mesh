Where did the first ancestors of mankind appear?

IAMGODIAM Knowledge Mesh · Urantia Book Study Guides · Paper 62 — The Dawn Races of Early Man · Part III — The History of Urantia · reviewed 2026-10-16

About one million years ago, the immediate ancestors of mankind appeared through three successive and sudden mutations from early lemur stock. Paper 62 traces this dawn line: the dawn mammals of Mesopotamia, the superior twins born to a mid-mammal pair, and the first creatures the Life Carriers recognized as truly intelligent and will-endowed — the threshold at which animal ancestry crossed over into human beginnings.

“ABOUT one million years ago the immediate ancestors of mankind made their appearance by three successive and sudden mutations stemming from early stock of the lemur type of placental mammal. The dominant factors of these early lemurs were derived from the western or later American group of the evolving life plasm. But before establishing the direct line of human ancestry, this strain was reinforced by contributions from the central life implantation evolved in Africa. The eastern life group contributed little or nothing to the actual production of the human species.”

What this paper teaches

The paper is careful about ancestry: the early lemurs behind the human line were not the offspring of the modern lemur, nor directly related to the gibbons and apes of Eurasia and northern Africa, though all sprang from a common ancestor long extinct. It follows the dawn mammals as they appeared suddenly a little more than a million years ago, describes their keen minds, their treetop shelters, and their tribal, combative temper, and ends with the Life Carriers’ own testimony — after their long vigil since planting the life plasm, the first intelligent beings appeared, and they did not have to wait long for more.

Why it matters

Paper 62 refuses every ladder that ranks peoples by nearness to the animal. The human line, it says, was reinforced from the central life implantation evolved in Africa, and the dawn stock itself was a blend long before any modern people existed. Fear, in this telling, is not a shameful inheritance but the first teacher of survival — the caution that built shelters and kept the line alive. To study these dawn races honestly is to remember that every nation on earth descends from the same small, frightened, determined ancestors, and none may claim a purer beginning.
