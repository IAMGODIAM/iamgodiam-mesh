How did Jesus’ final appearances and ascension unfold?

Paper 193 brings the resurrection appearances to their close. Jesus instructs a gathered company in Nicodemus’s courtyard, appears to Nalda and Samaritan believers at Jacob’s well, appears again at Tyre, and then makes his last appearance on earth before the farewell gathering on Mount Olivet. After the ascension, the believers regroup and begin calling the leading disciples together. The circle keeps widening even as it ends.

What this paper teaches

The sixteenth manifestation gathers the eleven apostles, the women’s corps and their associates, and about fifty other leading disciples, including Greeks, for an evening of instruction that leaves them discussing the Master’s admonitions until near daybreak. At Sychar the seventeenth appearance reaches Nalda and about seventy-five Samaritans, who hurry to nearby towns with the news. At Tyre the eighteenth appearance closes a believers’ meeting, and witnesses carry the story onward to Sidon, Antioch, and Damascus; friends who could not readily recognize the morontia form knew his personality when he spoke. The final section turns to the last appearance, the farewell warning that uses Judas’s loss as a solemn caution, the quiet company on Mount Olivet, and the summons to assemble the leading disciples at the home of Mary Mark.

Why it matters

The farewell circle is wider than the frightened room where this story resumed. Samaritans at a well, Greeks among the Jerusalem disciples, women organized in their own corps, and believers in Tyre all stand inside the final appearances, and the news travels by their testimony to cities beyond. That widening matters for any people told they are peripheral to sacred history. The paper also refuses a sentimental ending: Judas is remembered as warning, grief is still present on Olivet, and the community must gather itself deliberately after its leader departs.
