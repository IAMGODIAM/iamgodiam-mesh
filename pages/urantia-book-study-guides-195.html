What this paper teaches

The paper’s arc is expansion followed by testing. Hellenization begins in earnest when Paul tells the Athenians of the Unknown God at the Areopagus; the Romans take over Greek culture and put representative government in place of government by lot; Roman rule leaves Christians with one God but without empire. When the church becomes an adjunct to society and ally of politics, it shares the intellectual and spiritual decline of the dark ages. The twentieth century then brings materialistic panic, a run on the moral bank of the ages, secularism’s blight, and mechanistic theories, yet the paper insists the bank holds vast spiritual resources and urges readers not to overlook the river of truth in their heritage. It ends by granting Christianity’s great service and naming the deeper need: Jesus.

Why it matters

This is a sober paper for anyone who inherits a faith carried through empire. It remembers that the message first won the lower strata, people with the least protection, before it impressed the best minds of the empire; dignity traveled upward from below. It also warns that alliance with political power is never free: a church that becomes society’s adjunct shares society’s decline. Yet the paper is not despairing. The moral bank has vast resources, the river of truth still runs through barren centuries, and a people who guard their spiritual heritage can outlast both materialism and secular contempt.
