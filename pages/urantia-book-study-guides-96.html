How did the Hebrews come to worship Yahweh as the one God?

IAMGODIAM Knowledge Mesh · Urantia Book Study Guides · Paper 96 — Yahweh-God of the Hebrews · Part III — The History of Urantia · reviewed 2026-10-16

Paper 96 explains how Yahweh, once merely the god of the southern Palestinian tribes associated with Mount Horeb, became the sublime Lord God of Israel. Humanity's way of conceiving Deity, the paper says, moves from including all gods, to subordinating foreign gods to the tribal deity, to excluding all but the one God of supreme value. The Hebrew synthesis drew on the Salem religion preserved by the Kenites, on Egyptian moral teaching, and on Moses, whose exodus leadership began the elevation of the concept.

“The Salem religion was revered as a tradition by the Kenites and several other Canaanite tribes. And this was one of the purposes of Melchizedek’s incarnation: That a religion of one God should be so fostered as to prepare the way for the earth bestowal of a Son of that one God. Michael could hardly come to Urantia until there existed a people believing in the Universal Father among whom he could appear.”

“The early Semites regarded everything as being indwelt by a spirit. There were spirits of the animal and vegetable worlds; annual spirits, the lord of progeny; spirits of fire, water, and air; a veritable pantheon of spirits to be feared and worshiped. And the teaching of Melchizedek regarding a Universal Creator never fully destroyed the belief in these subordinate spirits or nature gods.”

What this paper teaches

The paper defines its Deity titles carefully to prevent confusion: Yahweh of the southern tribes, El Elyon the Most High of Salem, and the many nature spirits the early Semites saw indwelling animals, plants, fire, water, and air. It traces the Hebrews' uneven progress from polytheism through henotheism to monotheism — a path with many retrogressions. Then the story turns to Moses: the beginning of the Hebrew concept of a Supreme Creator dates from the departure from Egypt under his leadership, and the evolution of his teaching has influenced almost half the world. The paper closes with the deterioration after Moses' death and the loose settlement in Palestine.

Why it matters

Buried in this paper is a sentence that reframes who carried God's truth: the Salem religion survived among the Kenites — Canaanite peoples on the margins of the biblical story — and preserving it was one purpose of Melchizedek's coming. Truth endured because outsider communities kept it. The paper also insists the Hebrew path to one God was not a straight line but a history of advance and retrogression, led out of Egypt by a liberator who joined military leadership, social organization, and religious teaching in one person. A people's God-concept, like a people, grows through struggle — and exodus is where that growth begins.
