Who was John the Baptist?

IAMGODIAM Knowledge Mesh · Urantia Book Study Guides · Paper 135 — John the Baptist · Part IV — The Life and Teachings of Jesus · reviewed 2026-10-16

Paper 135 gives the forerunner his own biography. John was born March 25, 7 B.C., after Gabriel's promise to his mother Elizabeth — a promise she kept secret for five months, and that his father Zacharias believed fully only after an unusual dream. Aside from that visit and dream, the paper insists, nothing unusual or supernatural attended his birth. He grew up an only child on a small sheep farm, trained carefully at home by two priestly parents, and in time took the lifelong Nazarite vow.

“JOHN the Baptist was born March 25, 7 B.C., in accordance with the promise that Gabriel made to Elizabeth in June of the previous year. For five months Elizabeth kept secret Gabriel’s visitation; and when she told her husband, Zacharias, he was greatly troubled and fully believed her narrative only after he had an unusual dream about six weeks before the birth of John. Excepting the visit of Gabriel to Elizabeth and the dream of Zacharias, there was nothing unusual or supernatural connected with the birth of John the Baptist.”

“John took the same life vows that had been administered to his illustrious predecessors, Samson and the prophet Samuel. A life Nazarite was looked upon as a sanctified and holy personality. The Jews regarded a Nazarite with almost the respect and veneration accorded the high priest, and this was not strange since Nazarites of lifelong consecration were the only persons, except high priests, who were ever permitted to enter the holy of holies in the temple.”

What this paper teaches

The paper follows John's whole arc. At fourteen he took the formal Nazarite vow, joining the line of Samson and Samuel. After Zacharias died, John withdrew to a shepherd's life along a brook valley, building stone shelters and night corrals for his herds of sheep and goats. His preaching drew crowds in a generation hungry for the promised deliverer, though John himself still held confused ideas about the coming kingdom and its king. He baptized Jesus, pointed his own disciples toward him, then retraced his steps southward into Perea. Arrested there, he endured a lonely, bitter imprisonment in the fortress of Machaerus, where he was executed.

Why it matters

John matters because he prepared a road he would not walk to its end. He was a priest's son who chose the wilderness over the temple courts, a preacher whose own picture of the kingdom stayed confused even as he pointed beyond himself. The paper does not hide his prison bitterness or his doubts. For liberation work, that honesty is gold: the forerunner's faithfulness is measured not by his clarity or his reward but by his willingness to decrease when the one he announced had come. That is the forerunner's whole glory.
