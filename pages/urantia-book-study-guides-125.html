What happened when Jesus first visited Jerusalem?

IAMGODIAM Knowledge Mesh · Urantia Book Study Guides · Paper 125 — Jesus at Jerusalem · Part IV — The Life and Teachings of Jesus · reviewed 2026-10-16

Paper 125 calls Jesus' first remembered visit to Jerusalem the most engaging, humanly thrilling event of his youth. He virtually refused to go unless his mother accompanied them, so the Passover company held the largest proportion of women ever to go up from Nazareth. In the temple he attended the discussions by himself, questioned freely with the scribes and teachers, and enjoyed his first complete freedom from responsibility — while his parents, missing him, searched in growing distress.

“Everywhere Jesus went throughout the temple courts, he was shocked and sickened by the spirit of irreverence which he observed. He deemed the conduct of the temple throngs to be inconsistent with their presence in “his Father’s house.” But he received the shock of his young life when his father escorted him into the court of the gentiles with its noisy jargon, loud talking and cursing, mingled indiscriminately with the bleating of sheep and the babble of noises which betrayed the presence of the money-changers and the vendors of sacrificial animals and sundry other commercial commodities.”

What this paper teaches

The paper walks through the visit in detail. It describes Jesus shocked and sickened by the irreverence of the temple courts and the court of the gentiles, with its money-changers, sacrificial vendors, and frivolous courtesans, and his free words to Joseph about the profanation. It tells of the Passover kept with Simon of Bethany's household, the arranged rendezvous the party missed, and Jesus remaining in the temple through the afternoon. It follows his third day with the scribes and teachers as spectators gathered to watch a Galilean lad confound the wise men, and his strange unmindfulness of his parents' worry when they found him.

Why it matters

A twelve-year-old from the provinces walked into the center of religious power and named its corruption — commerce and irreverence in his Father's house. And when this son spoke of the love of God and the mercifulness of the Father in heaven, Joseph and Mary held their peace; never again did they seek to change his mind. This paper blesses the young who see clearly and speak honestly, and it honors elders who learn to listen. A child's conviction, plainly spoken at the center of things, can instruct the whole household — and the whole faith.
