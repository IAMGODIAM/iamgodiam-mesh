What happened during Jesus' sojourn at Tyre and Sidon?

Paper 156 carries Jesus and his associates beyond Jewish territory to the Phoenician coast. Lodged at Sidon in the home of Karuska, a former patient of the Bethsaida hospital, they rest over the Sabbath — and a Syrian mother, Norana, brings her afflicted daughter and refuses to leave without her healing. From Sidon the party travels the coast cities, teaching in Tyre from July 11 to 24 before departing for Ptolemais.

“ON FRIDAY afternoon, June 10, Jesus and his associates arrived in the environs of Sidon, where they stopped at the home of a well-to-do woman who had been a patient in the Bethsaida hospital during the times when Jesus was at the height of his popular favor. The evangelists and the apostles were lodged with her friends in the immediate neighborhood, and they rested over the Sabbath day amid these refreshing surroundings. They spent almost two and one-half weeks in Sidon and vicinity before they prepared to visit the coast cities to the north.”

What this paper teaches

The paper opens with almost two and one-half weeks spent in Sidon and vicinity, the company absorbed in meditation on the Master's discourses on religion. Near the home of Karuska lives the Syrian woman Norana, who has heard much of Jesus as a great healer and teacher; although Jesus has charged his associates to tell no one of his presence, she comes bringing her little daughter, and even Thomas cannot send her away. Leaving Sidon on Tuesday, June 28, the party goes up the coast to Porphyreon and Heldua, then teaches in Tyre from July 11 to July 24, where Jesus tells of the white lily rearing its pure head into the sunshine while its roots are grounded in the slime.

Why it matters

Norana is the heart of this paper, and the disciples nearly miss her. A foreign mother, told the Master needs rest, answers the gatekeepers with a rebuke that still stings: what has happened to you, his disciples, that you would send away those who come seeking your Master's help? Her faith shames the insiders, and her child's need, she says, would appeal to a Master who loves all men and dares even to heal the gentiles when they believe. This is the gospel crossing a border it was told to respect — carried not by policy but by a mother's refusal to be dismissed. The lily teaching belongs beside her: purity reared out of the slime, not protected from it.
