What happened at the ordination of the twelve?

IAMGODIAM Knowledge Mesh · Urantia Book Study Guides · Paper 140 — The Ordination of the Twelve · Part IV — The Life and Teachings of Jesus · reviewed 2026-10-16

Paper 140 records the ordination of the twelve as public preachers of the gospel of the kingdom, just before noon on Sunday, January 12, A.D. 27. Jesus gathered the apostles along the seashore two by two — Andrew and Peter, who were fishing near the shore, then James and John — and led them apart into the highlands north of Capernaum. For once, all twelve were silent; even Peter was in a reflective mood. The long-waited-for hour had come.

“Before the formal ordination service Jesus spoke to the twelve as they were seated about him: “My brethren, this hour of the kingdom has come. I have brought you apart here with me to present you to the Father as ambassadors of the kingdom. Some of you heard me speak of this kingdom in the synagogue when you first were called. Each of you has learned more about the Father’s kingdom since you have been with me working in the cities around about the Sea of Galilee. But just now I have something more to tell you concerning this kingdom.”

What this paper teaches

Before the formal service, Jesus spoke to the twelve seated about him: this hour of the kingdom had come, and the new kingdom set up in the hearts of God's children would be an everlasting dominion — yet for those who enter it, a severe test is set, and faith alone passes through its portals. The twelve then knelt in a circle about him for the ordination prayer. The paper pauses to correct a label: the so-called Sermon on the Mount is not the gospel of Jesus but an ordination charge, teaching fatherly love rather than brotherly love. Afterward came a simple meal at Zebedee's home, a late breakfast commission, and a Sabbath return to the highland.

Why it matters

The ordination turns on being before doing. Jesus' last long teaching in this paper tries to show the twelve what they must be, not what they must do — an order the church has reversed ever since, and activist movements with it. The charge they received was never a technique: fatherly love, a severe test, faith alone through the portals. For a people often handed religion as respectability or as workload, this paper restores the dignity of character: heaven first forms ambassadors who are separate and distinct, then sends them. Formation is the credential.
