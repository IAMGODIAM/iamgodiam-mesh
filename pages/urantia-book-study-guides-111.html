What this paper teaches

The paper surveys how religions have long sensed an inner, enduring presence — the Hindu atman, the Chinese yang and yin, the Egyptian and African ka and ba — before defining the soul as the morontia child of Adjuster and mind. It describes mind's levels, with totality dominant over the parts, and recognition as the fitting of sense impressions into memory patterns. Human mistakes, it says, may delay the soul's evolution but cannot inhibit it once the Adjuster has initiated it. The paper closes on the adventure ahead: uncertainty in time joined to security in eternity.

Why it matters

Begin with the claim itself: a fragment of God indwells the human mind — every human mind, without exception or ranking. For people whose worth has been measured by other people's instruments, this paper relocates the measure. The soul cannot be comprehended by science or philosophy, it says; it is known by cosmic insight and spiritual discovery, which no gatekeeper controls. And its growth cannot be inhibited by human error once begun. A community can therefore treat every person's inner life as sacred ground under construction — delayed, perhaps, by circumstance, but never disqualified.
