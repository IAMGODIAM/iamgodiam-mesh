How can an individual person know God?

IAMGODIAM Knowledge Mesh · Urantia Book Study Guides · Paper 5 — God's Relation to the Individual · Part I — The Central and Superuniverses · reviewed 2026-10-16

Paper 5 narrows the lens from the universe to a single human life. If the finite mind cannot grasp how a majestic God could fraternize with an individual creature, the paper answers, it can rest on a fact closer than thought: an actual fragment of the living God — a Thought Adjuster — resides within the intellect of every normal-minded, morally conscious mortal. A person does not have to travel outward to find God; communion begins with the inner experience of that indwelling spiritual presence.

“The mortals of the realms of time and space may differ greatly in innate abilities and intellectual endowment, they may enjoy environments exceptionally favorable to social advancement and moral progress, or they may suffer from the lack of almost every human aid to culture and supposed advancement in the arts of civilization; but the possibilities for spiritual progress in the ascension career are equal to all; increasing levels of spiritual insight and cosmic meanings are attained quite independently of all such sociomoral differentials of the diversified material environments on the evolutionary worlds.”

“However Urantia mortals may differ in their intellectual, social, economic, and even moral opportunities and endowments, forget not that their spiritual endowment is uniform and unique. They all enjoy the same divine presence of the gift from the Father, and they are all equally privileged to seek intimate personal communion with this indwelling spirit of divine origin, while they may all equally choose to accept the uniform spiritual leading of these Mystery Monitors.”

What this paper teaches

The paper first explains why the approach feels impossible: the barrier is our finiteness and material limitation, not the Father's aloofness. It then follows God's presence downward — the physical presence of the Infinite as the reality of the material universe, the Paradise Deities relating to Urantia's beings as three distinct persons, and finally the fragment of God indwelling each mind. Two later sections trace morality itself: the religions of evolution are driven forward by fear, while morality proper has its origin in the reason of self-consciousness — superanimal, though wholly evolutionary. It ends with the Father as the God of personalities.

Why it matters

This may be the most leveling paper in the book's first part. Mortals differ in intellect, wealth, schooling, and every social advantage — but their spiritual endowment, it says, is uniform and unique. The possibilities for spiritual progress are equal to all, attained quite independently of the material environments of the evolutionary worlds. A person raised with every human aid to culture and a person denied nearly all of them stand before God with the same indwelling gift and the same open door. No hierarchy built on earth — of race, class, or nation — survives that sentence. Heaven's distribution is radically equal.
