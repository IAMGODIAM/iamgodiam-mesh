How did the six races of color arise on Urantia?

IAMGODIAM Knowledge Mesh · Urantia Book Study Guides · Paper 64 — The Evolutionary Races of Color · Part III — The History of Urantia · reviewed 2026-10-16

Paper 64 tells the story of the evolutionary races of Urantia across almost one million years — from Andon and Fonta, through the rise and near-disappearance of their culture, to the appearance of the six colored races of the Sangik family and the coming of the Planetary Prince. Along the way it follows the Foxhall peoples in the west, another struggling center of culture in the east, the far-traveling Neanderthalers, and the great racial struggle of the Badonan tribes in India.

“The human race is almost one million years old, and the first half of its story roughly corresponds to the pre-Planetary Prince days of Urantia. The latter half of the history of mankind begins at the time of the arrival of the Planetary Prince and the appearance of the six colored races and roughly corresponds to the period commonly regarded as the Old Stone Age.”

“These Andonites avoided the forests in contrast with the habits of their nonhuman relatives. In the forests man has always deteriorated; human evolution has made progress only in the open and in the higher latitudes. The cold and hunger of the open lands stimulate action, invention, and resourcefulness. While these Andonic tribes were developing the pioneers of the present human race amidst the hardships and privations of these rugged northern climes, their backward cousins were luxuriating in the southern tropical forests of the land of their early common origin.”

What this paper teaches

The paper reckons the human race at almost one million years old, its first half belonging to the days before the Planetary Prince. It records a low ebb 900,000 years ago, when the arts of Andon and Fonta and the culture of Onagar were vanishing and even flintworking stood at its worst. It explains that on an average evolutionary planet the six races of color appear one by one, the red man first, roaming the world for ages before the others — and that on Urantia the Sangik children appeared together. It closes as the colored races multiply and expand while the fifth glacier advances southward.

Why it matters

This paper must be read with clear eyes. It presents the races of color as one family of one stock — the Sangik children of a single ancestry — appearing on a world where, unusually, they arose together rather than in sequence. Read in the Black-first study tradition, the ground of the paper is kinship, not ranking: every race in its account struggles, wanders, builds, and nearly vanishes at some point, the Andonites included. The lesson to carry forward is the shared million-year road — no people’s dignity is older, newer, or more earned than another’s.
