What did Jesus teach in his last week at Pella?

Paper 169 records the last week at Pella, with Jesus preaching every afternoon to the crowds and answering questions each night for the apostles and advanced disciples. The camp is still stirred by word of the resurrection of Lazarus, and the Pharisees and chief priests are crystallizing their charges. The week's centerpiece is Thursday's sermon on the grace of salvation, retelling the lost sheep and the lost coin and adding Jesus' favorite parable of the prodigal son.

“LATE on Monday evening, March 6, Jesus and the ten apostles arrived at the Pella camp. This was the last week of Jesus’ sojourn there, and he was very active in teaching the multitude and instructing the apostles. He preached every afternoon to the crowds and each night answered questions for the apostles and certain of the more advanced disciples residing at the camp.”

“You have been taught that divine acceptance comes after your repentance and as a result of all your works of sacrifice and penitence, but I assure you that the Father accepts you even before you have repented and sends the Son and his associates to find you and bring you, with rejoicing, back to the fold, the kingdom of sonship and spiritual progress. You are all like sheep which have gone astray, and I have come to seek and to save those who are lost.”

What this paper teaches

The sermon insists that the Father accepts the sinner before repentance, sending the Son and his associates to seek and save the lost; the prodigal story that follows tells of a thoughtless son who left his father's house with premeditation, unlike the sheep that strayed without intention. Later evenings take up Simon Zelotes' question about Jesus' saying that the children of the world are often wiser in their generation than the children of the kingdom. One meeting grows so noisy with dispute that Simon Peter stands and restores order. And through the week Jesus keeps struggling to make the apostles see that, while they proclaim the kingdom of God, the Father in heaven is not a king.

Why it matters

The grace preached this week overturns the ledger most religion keeps. Acceptance does not wait for repentance, sacrifice, or penitence to be completed first; the Father receives the lost beforehand and sends seekers out after them with rejoicing. That is a word for everyone who has been told to make themselves worthy before they may come home. The lost sheep did not find its own way back, and the prodigal was loved before he ever turned around. In this teaching, dignity is not earned at the door — the door is opened from the Father's side.
