Which Sons of God are born in the local universe itself?

IAMGODIAM Knowledge Mesh · Urantia Book Study Guides · Paper 35 — The Local Universe Sons of God · Part II — The Local Universe · reviewed 2026-10-16

Alongside the Paradise Sons who visit it, a local universe raises Sons of its own — offspring of the Creator Son in varied partnership with the Universe Mother Spirit. First come the Melchizedeks, the first order of divine Sons near enough to creature life to minister directly to mortal uplift. Then the Vorondadeks, at least three of whom rule each of the hundred constellations; then the Lanonandeks, the continuous rulers of the planets and the rotating sovereigns of the systems. Together with the versatile Life Carriers, they form the descending sonship of Nebadon, bending downward toward the worlds.

What this paper teaches

The Melchizedeks come first in the story: the original Father Melchizedek arises from a new creative union of the Son and the Creative Spirit, then collaborates with them to bring the whole order into existence, afterward serving as Gabriel's first executive associate — Gabriel handles universe policies while Melchizedek oversees practical procedures, special commissions, and the supervision of ascending mortals' morontia careers. The order keeps a world of its own near Salvington. The Vorondadeks and Lanonandeks are remembered in the Salvington circuit itself, where whole groups of seven worlds — the Vorondadek planets, and the Lanonandek cluster with its forty-two satellites — serve as their administrative spheres.

Why it matters

The Melchizedeks show what high office looks like in this universe: they go out in small groups as mobile, advisory review courts — taking depositions, receiving suggestions, acting as counselors — helping to settle the serious differences that arise in the evolutionary domains. Authority arrives as a listening commission, not an occupying force. And these are the Sons deemed closest to the ground, the first to approach lowly creature life closely enough to lift it without needing to be born into it. Rank, here, is measured by nearness to the people served — the higher the Son, the lower he bends.
