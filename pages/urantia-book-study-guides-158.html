What this paper teaches

The paper recounts that Jesus had been summoned up the mountain, apart by himself, for matters concerning the progress of his bestowal — an extraordinary event timed to occur while the apostles were in the lands of the gentiles, on a mountain of the gentiles. At lunch on the way up, he tells the three something of his experience in the hills east of Jordan after his baptism. Peter asks how long they remain away from their brethren, and Jesus answers: until you shall see the glory of the Son of Man. What the three witness is a fleeting glimpse of a celestial pageant. The company then remains overnight with Celsus, departs Wednesday for Magadan Park, and enters Capernaum at twilight by unfrequented roads.

Why it matters

The mountaintop is not an escape; it is a preparation, and Jesus says so by his answer — they stay until the three see glory and know that whatsoever he has declared to them is true. Glory here is not spectacle for its own sake: it steadies witnesses for the road back down, where the nine who tarried behind wait, more than relieved to welcome him, needing the encouragement the three carry down. Communities shaped by struggle know both altitudes — the fleeting glimpse that confirms the calling, and the valley where the work resumes. This paper refuses to let either one be the whole story: the pageant is real, and so is the descent.
