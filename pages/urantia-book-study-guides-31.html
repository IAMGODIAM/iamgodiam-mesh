What is the Corps of the Finality?

IAMGODIAM Knowledge Mesh · Urantia Book Study Guides · Paper 31 — The Corps of the Finality · Part I — The Central and Superuniverses · reviewed 2026-10-16

The Corps of Mortal Finaliters is the present known destination of ascending mortals who have fused with their indwelling Adjusters and completed the long climb to Paradise. It is not mortals alone: Havona natives, guardian seraphim who rose with their human wards, midway creatures, and Adamic citizens all take the finaliter oath. Organized in companies of 999 permanent members, and wholly self-governing under the Paradise Trinity, the corps now serves throughout the universes of time while awaiting a future assignment in the universes of outer space.

“These six groups of glorified beings compose this unique body of eternal destiny. We think we know their future work, but we are not certain. While the Corps of the Mortal Finality is mobilizing on Paradise, and while they now so extensively minister to the universes of space and administer the worlds settled in light and life, their future destination must be the now-organizing universes of outer space. At least that is the conjecture of Uversa.”

What this paper teaches

The paper builds the corps group by group. Ascendant Adjuster-fused mortals form its bulk, joined by Havona natives who serve as teachers in the pilgrim-training schools, by faithful seraphic guardians permitted to ascend with their human wards, by Adamic citizens released on petition from permanent citizenship, and by midway creatures, who seldom stay on their native worlds once those worlds settle in light and life. Wherever Gravity Messengers function, the finaliters are in command. On Paradise, part of a finaliter's experience is striving to comprehend more than a thousand groups of transcendental supercitizens, and the senior Master Architect oversees all seven Corps of the Finality.

Why it matters

Notice who arrives together at the summit: mortals who began on evolutionary worlds, the angels who guarded them, citizens of the central universe itself. The corps receives them, the text says, in equality — yet that equality does not erase anyone's individuality or personal identity. And the destination of the long climb is not obedience school: the finaliters are wholly self-governing, choose their own leaders, and answer by oath to the Paradise Trinity alone, beyond the reach of any outside influence on their policies. The end of ascension, in this telling, is a free people trusted with the universes to come.
