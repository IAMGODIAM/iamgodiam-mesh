Why did Jesus tarry in Galilee after his baptism?

Paper 137 explains the pause after the baptism. Coming down from the hills on Saturday morning, February 23, A.D. 26, Jesus rejoined John's company at Pella and spent the day mingling with the multitude — pausing even to carry an injured lad home to his parents. John's leading disciples began to transfer to him; the wedding at Cana followed; and then, instead of launching a campaign, Jesus settled at Capernaum for four long months of tarrying, holding over one hundred earnest sessions with his six associates and his brother James.

What this paper teaches

The paper lingers over beginnings. At Pella, John's disciples Andrew and Simon, then James and John, attached themselves to Jesus — and from that day the term apostle distinguished his chosen advisers from the multitude of disciples. The party went up to the wedding at Cana, where nearly a thousand guests gathered. Jesus' first public appearance after baptism came in the Capernaum synagogue on Sabbath, March 2. Then came the tarrying time itself: March through June of waiting, teaching, and sifting, ending with a second synagogue sermon shortly after John was imprisoned, just before the first preaching tour.

Why it matters

The most radical thing in this paper is the waiting. Fresh from baptism and a crowded wedding feast, with crowds available and momentum building, Jesus chose four months of unhurried sessions with a handful of men — most of it cheerful and joyous, the paper says. Movements are usually killed by haste: by launching before the people are formed. The tarrying time teaches the opposite discipline. A small company, deeply taught and genuinely glad, is worth more than a multitude briefly stirred. Formation before expansion — that is the strategy hidden in the pause.
