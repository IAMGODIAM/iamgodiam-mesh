What happened on Jesus' final journey to Jerusalem?

Paper 171 follows the last journey: the day after his sermon on “The Kingdom of Heaven,” Jesus announced that he and the apostles would leave for the Passover at Jerusalem, visiting the cities of southern Perea on the way. Nearly a thousand followers set out with him, though many still imagined he was going to claim a throne. The paper traces the slow, deliberate road south — through the towns of the seventy, to Jericho and Zaccheus — while Jesus taught the cost of discipleship to people who did not yet understand where the road ended.

What this paper teaches

The paper is a travel record with a shadow over it. It opens with the announcement at Pella and the followers' fixed idea that Jesus was going to Jerusalem to establish a nationalistic kingdom. At the Jordan ford, after the sermon on “Counting the Cost,” about half the company turned back toward Jerusalem. The rest followed for more than two weeks through the towns where Abner's associates and the seventy had labored, camping at Livias before reaching Jericho on March 30. There Jesus lodged with Zaccheus, taught his household the gospel of the kingdom, and — the paper notes — spread good cheer everywhere he went.

Why it matters

This paper honors a hard kind of honesty. Jesus does not correct his followers' hopes by crushing them; he teaches counting the cost and lets each person decide whether to keep walking. A people longing for liberation can mistake the form it will take — throne, crown, national triumph — yet the longing itself is not mocked here. What the paper asks of any movement is the discipline the crowd at the ford lacked or found: to stay on the road after the sermon turns costly, and to carry good cheer, as Jesus did at Jericho, into the very towns that suffering has marked.
