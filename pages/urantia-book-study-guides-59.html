What was the marine-life era on Urantia?

The marine-life era covers about one quarter of Urantia's planetary history — the long age when all life lived in the seas. Paper 59 reckons the planet's billion-year history in five major eras and divides the marine age into six periods, tracing life from the inland seas and shorelines, through the appearance of fish, and on toward the first land animals. It is the story of life learning the waters before it ever touched dry ground.

“By the dawn of this period of relative quiet on the earth’s surface, life is confined to the various inland seas and the oceanic shore line; as yet no form of land organism has evolved. Primitive marine animals are well established and are prepared for the next evolutionary development. Amebas are typical survivors of this initial stage of animal life, having made their appearance toward the close of the preceding transition period.”

What this paper teaches

The paper sets the frame first: the prelife era spans the initial 450,000,000 years, and the life-dawn era the next 150,000,000, before marine life proper begins. In the first marine period, life is confined to inland seas and the oceanic shoreline; no land organism has yet evolved, and amebas appear as typical survivors of the transition. Land elevation and sinking proceed gradually, with little volcanic action; 300,000,000 years ago another great period of submergence begins. Through the agelong struggle between land and water the sea is long victorious, the appearance of fish marks the apex of marine evolution, and the period closes by opening the transition to land animals.

Why it matters

Before anything walked, everything swam. This paper honors the humble beginnings of life on our world — amebas in inland seas, creatures with no land to stand on — as full chapters of the story, not embarrassments before the real history begins. The fossil record is even preserved in the rocks of North America, separated layer from layer, as if the earth itself kept receipts. Peoples whose origins are mocked as lowly should hear this plainly: creation does not skip the lowly stages, and survival through an age when the sea keeps winning is itself a kind of triumph.
