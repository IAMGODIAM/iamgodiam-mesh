What were Jesus' final admonitions and warnings to the apostles?

IAMGODIAM Knowledge Mesh · Urantia Book Study Guides · Paper 181 — Final Admonitions and Warnings · Part IV — The Life and Teachings of Jesus · reviewed 2026-10-16

After the farewell discourse, Jesus stayed with the eleven, recounting experiences that concerned them as a group and as individuals. The apostles were only beginning to grasp that he would leave them, and they bent his promise to return into the hope that he would come back after a brief absence to establish the kingdom. Into that misunderstanding Jesus began to speak his final admonitions and warnings: he would return not in the flesh but as a spirit indweller of every believer, and his peace would not resemble the satisfactions of the material world.

“After the farewell discourse had been discussed and had begun to settle down in their minds, Jesus again called the apostles to order and began the impartation of his final admonitions and warnings.”

“Jesus gives peace to his fellow doers of the will of God but not on the order of the joys and satisfactions of this material world. Unbelieving materialists and fatalists can hope to enjoy only two kinds of peace and soul comfort: Either they must be stoics, with steadfast resolution determined to face the inevitable and to endure the worst; or they must be optimists, ever indulging that hope which springs eternal in the human breast, vainly longing for a peace which never really comes.”

What this paper teaches

The paper opens with the apostles' misreading of the promise to return, then records the admonitions Jesus gave the eleven as a group. He tells them that, delivered from mortal nature, he will become a spiritual incarnation in the souls of all true believers through the Spirit of Truth, a well of living water springing up into eternal life. He charges them to follow him by living in the spirit of his teachings and the ideal of his life—the doing of the Father's will—rather than by imitating his earthly life. The closing section turns from the group to the apostles as individuals, completing his farewell instructions and final warnings to each of them.

Why it matters

A farewell like this refuses both despair and fantasy. Jesus distinguishes his peace from the stoic's grim endurance and the optimist's vain longing, then promises his followers a presence that no prison, exile, or empire can confiscate: his own spirit dwelling within each believer. For communities whose leaders have been taken by violence or distance, that is not small comfort. Authority here does not become a cult of the departed man; the charge is to do the Father's will, a practice any believer can carry into ordinary work, family, and resistance. The kingdom survives departure because it was never only in one body.
