How did Jesus train evangelists at Bethsaida?

IAMGODIAM Knowledge Mesh · Urantia Book Study Guides · Paper 148 — Training Evangelists at Bethsaida · Part IV — The Life and Teachings of Jesus · reviewed 2026-10-16

Paper 148 describes the five months Jesus and the apostles spent at Bethsaida in A.D. 28 running a seaside camp and a school for evangelists. The camp — five hundred to fifteen hundred truth seekers, healing candidates, and the curious — was run by David Zebedee, with the sick cared for under Elman, a Syrian physician. Peter directed the school, where students from every race and nation learned in the morning and taught crowds in the afternoon.

“Peter, James, and Andrew were the committee designated by Jesus to pass upon applicants for admission to the school of evangelists. All the races and nationalities of the Roman world and the East, as far as India, were represented among the students in this new school of the prophets. This school was conducted on the plan of learning and doing. What the students learned during the forenoon they taught to the assembly by the seaside during the afternoon. After supper they informally discussed both the learning of the forenoon and the teaching of the afternoon.”

“While Andrew continued in general charge of the apostolic activities, Peter was in full charge of the school of the evangelists. The apostles all did their share in teaching groups of evangelists each forenoon, and both teachers and pupils taught the people during the afternoons. After the evening meal, five nights a week, the apostles conducted question classes for the benefit of the evangelists. Once a week Jesus presided at this question hour, answering the holdover questions from previous sessions.”

What this paper teaches

From May 3 to October 3 the Zebedee home at Bethsaida anchored a tented city, a model of order and sanitation, self-sustaining under David Zebedee's management, and no one was turned away. Elman the Syrian physician, with a corps of young women and men, organized the kingdom's first hospital. Peter, with James and Andrew vetting applicants, ran the school of evangelists on a learn-and-do plan: forenoon learning became afternoon teaching by the seaside, with question classes after supper and Jesus presiding weekly. Jesus himself appeared publicly fewer than a dozen times, but held private garden interviews two evenings a week, fielding hard questions from Nathaniel and John about healing and innocent suffering. The camp disbanded after a final meeting, six Pharisees from Jerusalem in the front row.

Why it matters

This is institution-building with a conscience. The camp feeds and houses up to fifteen hundred people, treats the sick under a physician, keeps order — and turns no one away, self-sustaining. Its school dignifies the student: every race and nationality of the Roman world and the East, as far as India, sits in the same classes, and learning is honored by teaching. The hardest questions are not suppressed either; why the innocent suffer is asked in the garden and answered at length. A people building freedom can claim the pattern: organize carefully, care for the sick, teach everyone, and multiply teachers.
