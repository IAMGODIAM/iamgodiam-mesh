How did the Melchizedek teachings spread through the Levant?

IAMGODIAM Knowledge Mesh · Urantia Book Study Guides · Paper 95 — The Melchizedek Teachings in the Levant · Part III — The History of Urantia · reviewed 2026-10-16

Paper 95 traces the Salem missionaries through southwestern Asia — Palestine, Mesopotamia, Egypt, Iran, and Arabia — the homeland of the faiths of the Western world. Their good news of Machiventa's one God bore fruit in some lands and met frustration in others, sometimes through lack of wisdom and sometimes through circumstances beyond their control. The paper dwells longest on Egypt, where the teachings took their deepest root, and on the teacher Amenemope, before following the message east to Iran and south into Arabia.

“AS INDIA gave rise to many of the religions and philosophies of eastern Asia, so the Levant was the homeland of the faiths of the Occidental world. The Salem missionaries spread out all over southwestern Asia, through Palestine, Mesopotamia, Egypt, Iran, and Arabia, everywhere proclaiming the good news of the gospel of Machiventa Melchizedek. In some of these lands their teachings bore fruit; in others they met with varying success. Sometimes their failures were due to lack of wisdom, sometimes to circumstances beyond their control.”

What this paper teaches

The paper opens in Mesopotamia, where by 2000 B.C. the Sethite teachings had faded under Bedouin Semite and northern horsemen influences, yet the seventh-day observance never fully disappeared. It credits the Salem teachers with reducing Mesopotamia's many gods and exalting a triad reminiscent of Melchizedek's three-circle insignia, while admitting they never overcame the popularity of Ishtar. Egypt is the turning point: the original teachings took deepest root there, shaping social and ethical ideals later transmitted through Hebrews and Greeks, flowering in Amenemope — called by many the “son of man” — and revived again when a royal Egyptian woman embraced the faith through a Salemite physician.

Why it matters

This paper honors a truth liberation movements live by: seeds planted in one generation bear fruit in another, often far from where they were sown. Egypt received the Salem teaching most deeply, and through Egypt — and through the Hebrews and Greeks — its social and ethical ideals reached the whole Western world. Africa stands in this account not as a mission field at the margins but as the soil where the message rooted best. The paper is also honest that the missionaries sometimes failed through their own lack of wisdom — a word for every movement that carries good news: humility is part of the work.
