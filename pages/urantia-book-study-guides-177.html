How did Jesus spend the Wednesday rest day?

IAMGODIAM Knowledge Mesh · Urantia Book Study Guides · Paper 177 — Wednesday, the Rest Day · Part IV — The Life and Teachings of Jesus · reviewed 2026-10-16

Wednesday was the apostles' customary rest day, and on this last one Jesus kept it deliberately. After a late breakfast eaten in ominous silence, he told the twelve to rest, to think over all that had happened since they came to Jerusalem, and to grow in grace. Then he went off alone into the hills — except that young John Mark, carrying a basket of food and water, talked his way into coming along, and spent the day at the Master's side.

“As Jesus was about to take the lunch basket from John’s hand, the young man ventured to say: “But, Master, you may set the basket down while you turn aside to pray and go on without it. Besides, if I should go along to carry the lunch, you would be more free to worship, and I will surely be silent. I will ask no questions and will stay by the basket when you go apart by yourself to pray.”

“John Mark was thrilled by the memory of this day with Jesus in the hills, but he never forgot the Master’s final admonition, spoken just as they were about to return to the Gethsemane camp, when he said: “Well, John, we have had a good visit, a real day of rest, but see to it that you tell no man the things which I told you.” And John Mark never did reveal anything that transpired on this day which he spent with Jesus in the hills.”

What this paper teaches

The paper gives the day three centers. In the hills, Jesus talked freely with John Mark about this world and the next, comparing their childhoods, warning the lad not to be discouraged by coming events, and assuring him he would become a mighty messenger of the kingdom; the day ended with an admonition to tell no man what had been said. Back at camp, the apostles walked Mount Olivet and grew anxious for Jesus' return. And shortly after Jesus left, Judas Iscariot disappeared from among his brethren, not returning until late afternoon; that evening Jesus retold many things and cheered the company.

Why it matters

On the eve of the worst week of their lives, the command is rest. Jesus does not fill the silence with strategy; he orders reflection, and then spends his own quiet day not with the twelve but with a young helper who carried the lunch. The paper dignifies the unranked helper: John Mark's faithfulness was simply staying near — in hiding close by, sleeping only when Jesus slept. Movements are carried by such people, whose service looks like nothing and holds everything together. Rest, here, is not escape from the struggle; it is how the struggle is survived.
