What is the Paradise Trinity?

IAMGODIAM Knowledge Mesh · Urantia Book Study Guides · Paper 10 — The Paradise Trinity · Part I — The Central and Superuniverses · reviewed 2026-10-16

The Paradise Trinity is the eternal association of the three original, co-ordinate, and coexistent personalities of Deity: God the Father, God the Son, and God the Spirit. Paper 10 explains what this unity accomplishes — above all, it facilitates the Father's escape from personality absolutism, associating the limitless expression of his infinite personal will with the absoluteness of Deity. Through the Son and the Spirit and their universe children, the Father is liberated from the limitations that would otherwise come with primacy, perfection, and infinity itself.

“THE Paradise Trinity of eternal Deities facilitates the Father’s escape from personality absolutism. The Trinity perfectly associates the limitless expression of God’s infinite personal will with the absoluteness of Deity. The Eternal Son and the various Sons of divine origin, together with the Conjoint Actor and his universe children, effectively provide for the Father’s liberation from the limitations otherwise inherent in primacy, perfection, changelessness, eternity, universality, absoluteness, and infinity.”

What this paper teaches

The paper works the paradox from several sides. The Father, back in eternity, inaugurated a policy of profound self-distribution, and by the technique of trinitization he constitutes himself the Father of the very Son he divests himself in — so that there is only one Deity, yet three positive and divine personalizations of Deity. Of all absolute associations, this first triunity is unique as an exclusive association of personal Deity. The paper cautions that attributes belong to the Persons — it is hardly consistent to speak of the Trinity as having attributes — and it grounds all law in the First Source and Center, closing with the equality of the three Persons, who are one.

Why it matters

At the summit of this book's universe stands a community, not a solitary monarch — and the paper draws the moral itself: “Divine personality is not self-centered; self-distribution and sharing of personality characterize divine freewill selfhood.” The Father reserves to himself only the powers he finds impossible to delegate; everything shareable, he shares, disclosing his personality as a Father who gives being and equality of self to the Son and the Spirit. Creators, it says, are moved to share divinity with their universe children. If that is what ultimate power is like, then hoarding — of wealth, of authority, of worth — is not strength. It is a failure to resemble God.
