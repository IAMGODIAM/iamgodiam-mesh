What are the attributes of God?

IAMGODIAM Knowledge Mesh · Urantia Book Study Guides · Paper 3 — The Attributes of God · Part I — The Central and Superuniverses · reviewed 2026-10-16

Paper 3 takes up how God's attributes operate in the real universe: his omnipresence, omnipotence, and omniscience. The Father is everywhere present at the same time, all the universes know that “the Lord God omnipotent reigns,” and the divine mind is conscious of the thought of all creation. But the paper's emphasis is just as much on restraint — the Father bestows himself and his authority through his Creator Sons, delegating power without ever diminishing the wisdom and power stored in his own central personality.

“GOD is everywhere present; the Universal Father rules the circle of eternity. But he rules in the local universes in the persons of his Paradise Creator Sons, even as he bestows life through these Sons. “God has given us eternal life, and this life is in his Sons.” These Creator Sons of God are the personal expression of himself in the sectors of time and to the children of the whirling planets of the evolving universes of space.”

What this paper teaches

Three classic attributes structure the paper. Omnipresence: God alone can be in two places — in numberless places — at the same time, and his presence unceasingly patrols the master universe. Omnipotence: the affairs of this world and other worlds are divinely supervised, yet giving himself away to new universes in no way lessens his potential of power or store of wisdom. Omniscience: “God knows all things,” the divine mind conversant with the thought of all creation. The closing sections describe a sovereign who relinquishes authority and delegates power to his Sons, yet remains primal — his hand still on the mighty lever of universal circumstances.

Why it matters

Omnipresence is usually taught as an abstract doctrine; lived, it is a claim about place. If God alone can be in numberless places at once, then no place is godforsaken — not the block the city abandoned, not the cell, not the exile's road. The Psalmist's question hangs over the paper: “Whither shall I go from your spirit? or whither shall I flee from your presence?” There is nowhere to flee because there is nowhere he is not. And notice how this almighty God prefers to work: not by direct transmittal of force, but through persons — Sons sent into the sectors of time, close enough for the lowliest creatures to discern.
