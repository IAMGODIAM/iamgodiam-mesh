What this paper teaches

The paper opens by grounding the appearances: the power by which Jesus rose is the very gift of eternal life he bestows on kingdom believers, and believers will arise with the same type of morontia body—real forms, seen truly, not visions or hallucinations. It then surveys the appearances in order, among them the fifth manifestation, to some twenty-five women believers assembled at the home of Joseph of Arimathea, and the sixth, to about forty Greek believers at the home of Flavius. It closes on the road to Emmaus, where two brothers, shepherds who had kept the Passover in Jerusalem, walked west toward home.

Why it matters

The witness list is the teaching. The risen Jesus appears to women believers gathered in a house, to Greek believers across the ethnic line, and to two obscure shepherd brothers walking home from the feast—not first to the Sanhedrin, the governor, or the powerful. Testimony rises from the margins, as it so often has in the life of oppressed communities, where women's witness carried the faith while officials denied it. And the apostles' slowness is treated gently: faith here is not credulity but the fruit of evidence and lived experience, granted to doubters who stayed within reach of the story.
