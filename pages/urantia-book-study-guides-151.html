What this paper teaches

The paper opens with all the preaching groups regathered at Bethsaida, fishing on Thursday and Friday and hearing an aged Jew of Damascus at the synagogue on the Sabbath, while Jesus spends most of the day alone in the hills. That night he teaches for more than an hour on the mission of adversity and the spiritual value of disappointment. Jesus then begins the parable method, telling the parable of the sower; Peter and his group, convinced it is an allegory, ask for an explanation, and the apostles devote the whole next evening to discussing parables. The next day he teaches from the boat the parable of the good seed and the weeds, as the multitude keeps growing through the week.

Why it matters

Jesus teaches the mission of adversity in the shadow of rejection: his own hometown has just refused him, and his sadness is plain to the apostles. For communities that know rejection by the powers of their own place, this paper insists disappointment is not abandonment but material for instruction. The parable method carries the same wisdom: truth is spoken in stories the crowd can carry home, while its depths are unfolded to those who stay and ask. A people long practiced at hearing more than the surface says will recognize the method — the seed is sown widely, but it roots where the soil has been prepared.
