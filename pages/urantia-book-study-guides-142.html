What happened during the Passover at Jerusalem?

IAMGODIAM Knowledge Mesh · Urantia Book Study Guides · Paper 142 — The Passover at Jerusalem · Part IV — The Life and Teachings of Jesus · reviewed 2026-10-16

Paper 142 covers the month of April in Jerusalem, where Jesus and the apostles teach daily in the temple during Passover and withdraw each night to Bethany. Much of the work is private: secret interviews at the home of Flavius, a long talk with Jacob of Crete about the Father's love for the individual soul, and a night visit from Nicodemus of the Sanhedrin. By month's end, opposition forces a move south — but the message has already begun traveling to the ends of the empire.

“THE month of April Jesus and the apostles worked in Jerusalem, going out of the city each evening to spend the night at Bethany. Jesus himself spent one or two nights each week in Jerusalem at the home of Flavius, a Greek Jew, where many prominent Jews came in secret to interview him.”

What this paper teaches

Throughout April, Jesus or one of the apostles taught daily in the temple, with overflow groups conducted outside when crowds grew too great. Believers carried the news from this celebration to the uttermost parts of the Roman Empire and to the East, so the work was no longer confined to Palestine. The paper lingers on private encounters: Jacob, a wealthy Jewish trader from Crete, hears that the Father loves him as an individual soul, not just as one of Abraham's children; the apostles question Jesus about the Father in heaven; Flavius, a proselyte of the gate and lover of art, hosts secret interviews; and Nicodemus comes by night. A great temple sermon and a Wednesday's rest at Bethany precede the departure for Bethlehem and Hebron.

Why it matters

The heart of this paper is one sentence spoken to a trader from Crete: the Father's love is not only for all the children of Abraham, but for you, your individual soul. Against every system that values persons only as members of a class, a nation, or a market, the teaching insists the single soul is seen and loved. Note also where it happens — in the home of Flavius, a half-outsider who was neither circumcised nor baptized, and in a night conversation with a ruler still gathering courage. The kingdom advances through kitchens, courtyards, and whispered interviews as surely as through temple courts.
