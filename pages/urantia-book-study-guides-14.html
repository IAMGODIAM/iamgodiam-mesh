What is Havona, the central universe?

IAMGODIAM Knowledge Mesh · Urantia Book Study Guides · Paper 14 — The Central and Divine Universe · Part I — The Central and Superuniverses · reviewed 2026-10-16

Havona is the perfect and divine universe at the center of all creation — one billion spheres of unimagined beauty arranged in seven circuits around Paradise. Paper 14 presents Havona not as an evolutionary product but as a wholly created, eternal pattern: the core of perfection around which the evolving universes of time and space revolve, and the standard against which their long adventure is measured.

“THE perfect and divine universe occupies the center of all creation; it is the eternal core around which the vast creations of time and space revolve. Paradise is the gigantic nuclear Isle of absolute stability which rests motionless at the very heart of the magnificent eternal universe. This central planetary family is called Havona and is far-distant from the local universe of Nebadon. It is of enormous dimensions and almost unbelievable mass and consists of one billion spheres of unimagined beauty and superb grandeur, but the true magnitude of this vast creation is really beyond the understanding grasp of the human mind.”

What this paper teaches

Several of the paper's claims run against ordinary expectation. Spirit beings, it says, do not float in nebulous space — they are domiciled on actual spheres of a material nature, worlds as real as mortal worlds. The central universe has no government, because a perfect creation needs no administration imposed on it. Life on the Havona worlds takes seven basic forms, each existing in three distinct phases, and the range of activities across the seven circuits is enormous. Against all this settled perfection the paper sets our own world: on Urantia, mortals pass through a short and intense test during the initial life of material existence.

Why it matters

Havona matters to people who will never see it because it sets the terms of hope. If the center of creation is already perfect — settled, established, and beautiful beyond imagining — then the disorder of our worlds is not the deepest truth about reality. Our life here is described honestly as a short and intense test, but the test is not meaningless: the evolving universes exist to reproduce, in time and space, the completeness that Havona already is. A community fighting for justice can work with that picture: the pattern is real, the outcome is anchored, and brief lives spent moving toward it count eternally.
