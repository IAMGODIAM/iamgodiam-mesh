What does The Measure of the Wound document?

IAMGODIAM Knowledge Mesh · The Black Papers Series · Black Paper № I · published 2026 · reviewed 2026-10-15

The Measure of the Wound: A Sovereign Empirical Record of Black American Structural Distress, 1991–2024 is the first published Black Paper — a roughly 27,000-word, ten-chapter record by Israel Lee Armstead (E5 Enclave Inc., CC0) that measures Black American structural distress across a 33-year window, from federal data rather than anecdote. It is blockchain-sealed (ExodusV4 Token № 2, Base Mainnet) and archived on Zenodo.

~14,811 raw federal data points — Bureau of Labor Statistics, Census Bureau, National Center for Health Statistics, Bureau of Justice Statistics, Federal Reserve, USDA, SlaveVoyages — synthesized into an eight-pillar instrument of 1,574 observations.

The dataset · described in the paper as the largest unified open dataset documenting Black American structural conditions ever assembled by a Black-led organization

The FarmBlock Distress Index scores structural distress at tract level: 15,507 census tracts across 49 cities — the dataset layer beneath the paper, published open (CC0) for any researcher to reuse.

FarmBlock Distress Index · github.com/IAMGODIAM/farmblock-data

An instrument, not an argument

Most writing about Black American conditions argues from selected examples. This paper builds a measuring instrument instead: eight pillars — the economic, health, justice, and education pillars among them — each scored from primary federal series over the same 33-year window, then read together in a compound-distress analysis. The claim is not that conditions feel bad; it is that distress is structural, measurable, and persistent across every pillar at once, and that any honest policy conversation has to start from the measurement.

Corrections printed, not buried

Every derived statistic in the paper was recomputed through four successive review waves, and the corrections are published as Appendix H rather than silently absorbed — the record shows its own errata. The current edition (v1.4.5, 96 pages) is the publication package on Zenodo; the full text and chapter drafts are open on GitHub.
