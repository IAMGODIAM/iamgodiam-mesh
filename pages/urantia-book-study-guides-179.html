Why was the Last Supper held a day before Passover?

IAMGODIAM Knowledge Mesh · Urantia Book Study Guides · Paper 179 — The Last Supper · Part IV — The Life and Teachings of Jesus · reviewed 2026-10-16

The supper was held a full day early, and the paper explains why. Saturday's Passover supper would be eaten Friday night, and preparations had to begin by Thursday noon; but Jesus, apparently expecting arrest before Friday evening, called the twelve together on Thursday instead. The apostles were at a loss — some thought it merely a special occasion before the regular feast. What they found was an upper chamber fully furnished, bread, wine, water, and herbs in readiness, and no servants provided: the Master would host them himself.

“DURING the afternoon of this Thursday, when Philip reminded the Master about the approaching Passover and inquired concerning his plans for its celebration, he had in mind the Passover supper which was due to be eaten on the evening of the next day, Friday. It was the custom to begin the preparations for the celebration of the Passover not later than noon of the preceding day. And since the Jews reckoned the day as beginning at sunset, this meant that Saturday’s Passover supper would be eaten on Friday night, sometime before the midnight hour.”

Except for the end on which rested the bread and wine, this long table was surrounded by thirteen reclining couches, just such as would be provided for the celebration of the Passover in a well-to-do Jewish household.”

What this paper teaches

The paper lingers over the meal's details. The apostles knew Jesus had celebrated other Passovers without the lamb — when he was host, no lamb was served, since he took no part in the sacrificial services. Greeted by John Mark's parents, they went up to the chamber with its thirteen reclining couches around the long table. The opening moments were strained and silent; then came the section that begins with the custom of the host washing his hands, where the Master himself performs the service no servant was there to do. Under his cheerful demeanor the meal warmed into conversation, and at the third cup — the cup of blessing — Jesus rose and blessed it.

Why it matters

A table with thirteen couches and no servants: in this paper the host does the serving, and rank is turned upside down in the room where the movement's most sacred meal is born. The supper also keeps its own calendar — eaten a day early, outside the official schedule, because fidelity to love would not wait for the authorities' timetable. And there is no lamb on the Master's table: the remembrance he establishes is bread and cup, worship without blood sacrifice, a feast the poorest household can keep. Service, not status, sets the order of seating.
