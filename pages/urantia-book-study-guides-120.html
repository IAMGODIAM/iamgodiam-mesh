What this paper teaches

The paper presents the commissioning itself. Michael had completed six bestowals in the likeness of six differing orders of creatures; now, wholly subject to his Father's will — the one phase he had not yet undergone — he receives Immanuel's charge. Immanuel assumes unqualified jurisdiction of the universe for the incarnation and counsels him: grow up on Urantia as a child of the realm, complete a human education, live the life he has determined, end the sojourn, and ascend to receive supreme sovereignty. The paper adds the charge's motive: certain of Michael's own children had accused their Creator-father of selfishly seeking rulership, and this bestowal answers the insinuation.

Why it matters

The answer to the charge of tyranny is not an argument; it is an incarnation. Michael's sovereignty, the paper says, is supreme precisely because it is divested of all arbitrary assumptions — power acquired through actual experience in the likeness of the governed. And the form of his coming matters: not a conqueror descending on a capital, but a child of the realm, growing up under the same conditions as the people he made. For communities that know power only as something done to them, this paper imagines authority from below — authority that begins in a cradle, not a palace.
