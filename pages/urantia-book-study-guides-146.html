Where did the first preaching tour of Galilee go?

IAMGODIAM Knowledge Mesh · Urantia Book Study Guides · Paper 146 — First Preaching Tour of Galilee · Part IV — The Life and Teachings of Jesus · reviewed 2026-10-16

Paper 146 traces the first public preaching tour of Galilee, from mid-January to mid-March of A.D. 28. For the first time Jesus lets his associates preach without restraint, and the party — apostles and John's former apostles together — works through Rimmon, Jotapata, Ramah, Zebulun, Iron, Gischala, Chorazin, Madon, Cana, Nain, and Endor, among other towns. The tour mixes glad welcomes, a memorable debate with a Greek philosopher at Ramah, and crowds at Nain who came mainly seeking signs.

“THE first public preaching tour of Galilee began on Sunday, January 18, A.D. 28, and continued for about two months, ending with the return to Capernaum on March 17. On this tour Jesus and the twelve apostles, assisted by the former apostles of John, preached the gospel and baptized believers in Rimmon, Jotapata, Ramah, Zebulun, Iron, Gischala, Chorazin, Madon, Cana, Nain, and Endor. In these cities they tarried and taught, while in many other smaller towns they proclaimed the gospel of the kingdom as they passed through.”

What this paper teaches

This was the first time Jesus permitted his associates to preach without restraint; he cautioned them only to avoid Nazareth and be discreet through Capernaum and Tiberias. At Rimmon, once dedicated to a Babylonian air god, much time went to distinguishing the older Babylonian and Zoroastrian beliefs from the new gospel; Peter preached on Aaron and the golden calf, and Todan, who first heard the gospel there, later carried it beyond the Euphrates. The paper records the discourse to the twenty-four at Jotapata, the discussion at Ramah with an aged Greek philosopher who held science and philosophy sufficient, synagogue teaching at Iron, the wonder-seeking generation at Nain, and a few days' escape at Endor from healing crowds.

Why it matters

The tour is a coalition before it is anything else: John's apostles and Jesus' apostles preach side by side, one movement honoring two formations. At Rimmon the old faith is not mocked but patiently weighed against the new gospel — a model for meeting people's inherited beliefs with respect instead of contempt. And the message's farthest journey in this paper is made by Todan, an otherwise unknown hearer who carried it across the Euphrates. Liberation travels that way: not only through named leaders, but through ordinary listeners who take the word to places the tour will never reach.
