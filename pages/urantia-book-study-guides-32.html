How does a local universe come into being and grow?

IAMGODIAM Knowledge Mesh · Urantia Book Study Guides · Paper 32 — The Evolution of Local Universes · Part II — The Local Universe · reviewed 2026-10-16

A local universe is the handiwork of a Creator Son of the Paradise order of Michael: one hundred constellations, each of one hundred systems, each system ripening toward roughly a thousand inhabited worlds. Unlike Havona, which was made perfect at once, every local universe is evolutionary — its physical, intellectual, and spiritual life unfolds gradually under the Creator Son's plan, fully approved by the Paradise Trinity before he ever begins. And before the Son can organize anything, power directors must first mobilize the space-energies into literal suns and material spheres.

What this paper teaches

The paper follows a universe from raw space-force to living creation. The Paradise Master Force Organizers do the preuniverse work, then retire in favor of the superuniverse power directors when emergent energy becomes responsive to gravity; indeed, the Creator Sons are preceded in universe organization by the power directors and other beings of the Third Source and Center, and the material creations themselves were originally projected by the Paradise Architects of the Master Universe. Against all this gradual building stands one exception: Havona, the central universe, the only creation perfectly settled, made by the Father's thought and the Son's word. The paper closes by insisting the Father, having delegated so much, is no silent partner — and that a great and glorious purpose moves in the march of the universes.

Why it matters

Here, growth is not a flaw in the creation; it is the creation's method. A local universe is not expected to arrive finished — it ripens, sphere by sphere, toward a thousand inhabited worlds, and its creatures develop physically, intellectually, and spiritually along the path of gradual evolvement. For people and neighborhoods treated as problems because they are still becoming, this paper offers a different measure: slowness is built into the divine plan, foundations are laid by hands that then step back, and even the architects wait for energy to become responsive before they build.
