What happened during Jesus' last days at Capernaum?

IAMGODIAM Knowledge Mesh · Urantia Book Study Guides · Paper 154 — Last Days at Capernaum · Part IV — The Life and Teachings of Jesus · reviewed 2026-10-16

Paper 154 tells the end of the Capernaum era. While Jesus comforts his bewildered disciples, the Sanhedrin's commissioners press Herod Antipas at Tiberias to arrest him as a stirrer of rebellion — and Herod refuses. For a week Jesus holds intimate counsel with about a hundred tried and trusted followers at the Zebedee house, preparing them for the dispersion he knows is coming, before the synagogue is closed against him and he departs.

“From May 1 to May 7 Jesus held intimate counsel with his followers at the Zebedee house. Only the tried and trusted disciples were admitted to these conferences. At this time there were only about one hundred disciples who had the moral courage to brave the opposition of the Pharisees and openly declare their adherence to Jesus. With this group he held sessions morning, afternoon, and evening. Small companies of inquirers assembled each afternoon by the seaside, where some of the evangelists or apostles discoursed to them. These groups seldom numbered more than fifty.”

What this paper teaches

The paper moves between two councils. At Tiberias, Herod hears the charges yet refuses to take action against Jesus as a political offender, even when threatened with a report to Caesar. Meanwhile Jesus spends May 1 to May 7 in counsel at the Zebedee house, admitting only disciples with the moral courage to brave the Pharisees openly. On Friday the rulers of the Capernaum synagogue officially close the house to Jesus and all his followers, and Jairus resigns as chief ruler and openly aligns himself with Jesus. Jesus declares a week's holiday of rest, a second conference meets at Tiberias on May 16, fifty leading citizens debate "What shall we do with Jesus?" until after midnight, and on May 22 his earth family arrives as the departure comes.

Why it matters

When the institution closes its doors, this paper watches who walks out with the teacher. Jairus gives up the chief ruler's seat rather than keep a synagogue that has barred Jesus; about a hundred disciples find the moral courage to be counted openly when being counted costs something. And Jesus, facing dispersion, does something unexpected: he sends his troubled followers home to rest, to play or fish, and to speak words of encouragement to their loved ones. A hunted movement survives here not by frenzy but by counsel, rest, and the quiet protection of trusted messengers who keep watch while the leader walks the shore.
