What this paper teaches

The paper tours the continental nation's life in detail: a representative government with a centrally located national capital; a law against two families living under one roof; compulsory, coeducational schooling from ages five to eighteen, plus special schools beyond it; industry where capital and labor are adjusting to sincere co-operation; a determined effort to replace self-respect-destroying charity with dignified government-insurance guarantees of security in old age; and public office restricted to graduates of schools of statesmanship. It also describes their methods with crime, insanity, and degeneracy, and an order of trained 'guardians of civilization' commissioned from the federal military schools.

Why it matters

The detail that should stop a Black reader cold is the charity line: this people judged that relief which destroys self-respect is a failure, and replaced it with dignified guarantees of security in old age as a matter of right. That is the opposite of every system that makes the poor beg, perform gratitude, or submit to inspection to eat. Add universal free schooling to eighteen and leadership treated as a trained profession rather than an inheritance, and the paper becomes a measuring stick: a world with our same handicaps — rebellion, default, isolation — still chose dignity as policy.
