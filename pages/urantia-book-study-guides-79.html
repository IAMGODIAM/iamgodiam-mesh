How did the Andites spread across Asia?

IAMGODIAM Knowledge Mesh · Urantia Book Study Guides · Paper 79 — Andite Expansion in the Orient · Part III — The History of Urantia · reviewed 2026-10-16

Paper 79 opens with a declaration: Asia is the homeland of the human race. There Andon and Fonta were born, there their descendant Badonan founded a primitive culture center that lasted over half a million years, and there the Sangik peoples differentiated from the Andonic stock. The paper then traces the Andites — the blended descendants of Adam — as drought, trade, and migration carried them from Mesopotamia and Turkestan into India, the Levant, and China.

“ASIA is the homeland of the human race. It was on a southern peninsula of this continent that Andon and Fonta were born; in the highlands of what is now Afghanistan, their descendant Badonan founded a primitive center of culture that persisted for over one-half million years. Here at this eastern focus of the human race the Sangik peoples differentiated from the Andonic stock, and Asia was their first home, their first hunting ground, their first battlefield. Southwestern Asia witnessed the successive civilizations of Dalamatians, Nodites, Adamites, and Andites, and from these regions the potentials of modern civilization spread to the world.”

“When climatic conditions made hunting unprofitable for the migrating Andites, they did not follow the evolutionary course of the older races by becoming herders. Commerce and urban life made their appearance. From Egypt through Mesopotamia and Turkestan to the rivers of China and India, the more highly civilized tribes began to assemble in cities devoted to manufacture and trade. Adonia became the central Asian commercial metropolis, being located near the present city of Ashkhabad. Commerce in stone, metal, wood, and pottery was accelerated on both land and water.”

What this paper teaches

The paper maps the eastern expansion region by region. The heart of Eurasia stayed predominantly Andite for over twenty-five thousand years. India, it says, is the only locality where all the Urantia races were blended, the Andite invasions adding the last stock; the blending of Andite conquerors with the native stock produced the Dravidian people, and a second penetration came with the Aryan invasion. Eastward, the narrative belongs more to the primary Sangiks: the Chinese cleared the Andonites from eastern Asia's river valleys, and about fifteen thousand years ago Andites crossed the Ti Tao pass into the Yellow River valley.

Why it matters

This paper relocates the human story. Humanity's homeland, its first culture centers, and the blending of all its races happened in Asia — in Afghanistan's highlands, along India's rivers, in China's valleys — long before Europe enters the narrative. For readers formed by histories that begin civilization in the West, that is a correction with dignity in it: the peoples of Asia are not latecomers to the human story but its first home. And again the pattern is mixture — India's peoples formed where every race blended, and no stock in this telling advances alone.
