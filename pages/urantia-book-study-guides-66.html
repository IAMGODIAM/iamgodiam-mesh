What this paper teaches

The paper explains what a Planetary Prince’s arrival normally signifies — that will, the ability to choose the path of eternal survival, has developed in primitive man. It gives Caligastia’s biography: number 9,344 of the secondary order of Lanonandek Sons, experienced in local universe affairs, once attached to the Life Carrier advisers on Jerusem, and several times refused a planetary commission by the Constellation Fathers before finally being assigned to Urantia. It describes the profound impression the staff’s arrival made, the modest but awe-inspiring headquarters at Dalamatia, and the paper’s measure of culture: a world’s rate of cultural expansion is wholly determined by its inhabitants’ ability to comprehend new and advanced ideas.

Why it matters

Paper 66 is a study in entrusted power. Caligastia arrived with an enviable record — and with one flagged trait: a restlessness, a tendency to disagree with the established order, and a hunger for exactly this assignment that his superiors had repeatedly declined to grant. The paper lets us watch a gifted administrator carry a hidden flaw into the highest trust on the planet. For any people deciding whom to follow, the teaching is sober and useful: credentials are not character, and the warning signs around a leader are worth reading before the fall, not only after it.
