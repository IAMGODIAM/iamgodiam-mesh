The Universal Father is God as the First Source and Center of all things and beings — first a creator, then a controller, and finally an infinite upholder. What this paper teaches

The paper moves through the names of God, the nature of God, and the approach to God. Across the universes, the designations most often used name him First Source and Universe Center. It then insists that “God is spirit” — a universal spiritual presence — while admitting that the infinity of his perfection makes him eternally mystery. Two sections guard the balance: his magnitude must never eclipse his personality, and human personality is presented as the time-space image-shadow of the Creator's own personality. The paper closes where Jesus began, with “the living God” — not an abstraction, but a personal Deity, the Father in heaven.

Why it matters

A paper about one God “in the place of many gods” carries a quiet revolution: if there is a single First Source for every being, then no people can claim a private god who ranks them above another people. Human personality, this paper says, is the image-shadow of the Creator's personality — which means dignity is not granted by empires or markets; it is cast from above and cannot be revoked below. And the command “Be you perfect, even as I am perfect” is addressed to struggling creatures everywhere: growth toward Godlikeness is every person's birthright, not the privilege of the powerful.
