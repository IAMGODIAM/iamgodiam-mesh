What this paper teaches

The Paradise classification sorts beings first by origin: those of triune origin, created by all three Paradise Deities or attaining the Trinity; those of dual origin, arising from any two Deities or their descendants; and those of single origin. At the grand gatherings of the central and superuniverses, the assembled hosts are still grouped this way. The Uversa Register then presents its seven grand divisions of the divine family, and the paper notes the seven courtesy colonies that sojourn on the architectural spheres while executing their special missions. It closes by reserving an important place for the ascending pilgrims — the mortal survivors of time and space now accredited for the progressive ascent to Paradise.

Why it matters

A register is a moral document: it decides who counts. This one counts everybody — and, tellingly, it reserves its closing emphasis not for the highest orders of Paradise but for the ascending pilgrims, the evolutionary creatures of time and space on their way up. Peoples who have been struck from earthly censuses, miscounted, or counted as fractions can recognize what is being said: in the grand universe's own records, the mortal of a small world is registered, named, and given an important place. No one in this census is a rounding error, and the last entry is the pilgrim still climbing.
