How did prayer evolve?

IAMGODIAM Knowledge Mesh · Urantia Book Study Guides · Paper 91 — The Evolution of Prayer · Part III — The History of Urantia · reviewed 2026-10-16

Paper 91 traces prayer from its beginnings as nonreligious thinking-out-loud to a mature agency of religion. Before people had any clear concept of God, they prayed anyway — voicing wishes, asking luck, and speaking desires aloud. As self-consciousness awakened the awareness of others, and eventually of God, those monologues became genuine prayer. The paper follows prayer's growth through ancestor worship and ethical tests, asking what prayer can and cannot do, and closes with the laws of effective petition.

“PRAYER, as an agency of religion, evolved from previous nonreligious monologue and dialogue expressions. With the attainment of self-consciousness by primitive man there occurred the inevitable corollary of other-consciousness, the dual potential of social response and God recognition.”

“The function of early evolutionary religion is to conserve and augment the essential social, moral, and spiritual values which are slowly taking form. This mission of religion is not consciously observed by mankind, but it is chiefly effected by the function of prayer. The practice of prayer represents the unintended, but nonetheless personal and collective, effort of any group to secure (to actualize) this conservation of higher values. But for the safeguarding of prayer, all holy days would speedily revert to the status of mere holidays.”

What this paper teaches

The paper begins with prayer's prehistory: the earliest forms were not addressed to Deity at all, but were luck petitions, much like saying “Wish me luck” to a friend. It credits prayer with religion's quiet social mission — conserving and augmenting the moral and spiritual values slowly taking form in any group. From there it moves through the psychic circles of prayer, the ethics of prayer — no petition is ethical when it seeks selfish advantage over one's fellows — prayer's role in ancestor worship, its limits in affecting the physical environment, the promise and perils of mysticism, and the spontaneous, subconscious roots from which real prayer rises.

Why it matters

A people formed by prayer will recognize themselves in this paper's first insight: prayer was never the property of priests or temples — it began as ordinary people thinking out loud about their hopes. That democratizes the practice. The ethical test cuts just as deep: a prayer that asks advantage over a neighbor fails, which means prayer cannot be enlisted to bless domination. And the claim that prayer's real work is conserving a people's highest values dignifies the kitchens, fields, and church basements where oppressed communities have always kept their dignity alive by praying.
