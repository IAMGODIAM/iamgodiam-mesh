How did Jesus prepare himself in his early manhood?

Paper 128 shows Jesus entering his adult years having lived a normal and average human life on earth. With full self-consciousness he began in earnest the task of mastering the knowledge of his creatures' lives, thereby earning the right of rulership over his own universe. At home, his brothers and sisters were passing through their own adolescent trials, family finances were easing, and Jesus spent four months in Damascus as a merchant's guest.

What this paper teaches

The paper follows the young man's widening life. It describes his stupendous task: completing the experience of mastering the knowledge of the life of his lowest intelligent creatures, his two natures already combined into one — Jesus of Nazareth. It notes the years when his brothers and sisters faced the trials and readjustments of adolescence, and the year financial pressure relaxed as four were at work. It tells of his last four months of one year in Damascus as guest of the merchant he had met at Philadelphia, his first year of comparative freedom from family responsibility, the finishing of the regular schooling of all the children, and his growing consciousness of a wide range of potential power.

Why it matters

The paper insists on the plainness of Jesus' preparation: he obtained knowledge, gained experience, and combined these into wisdom, just as other mortals do, employing no agency beyond his human endowment as a son of Joseph and Mary. That is good news for people whose growth is slow, ordinary, and unglamorous. Mastery here was earned in family duty, in work, in patience with adolescent siblings, in years that looked like waiting. The One who calls us to grow first grew the same way himself — and dignifies every unhurried season of preparation.
