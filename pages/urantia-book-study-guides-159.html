What this paper teaches

The paper follows the tour city by city. One evening at Hippos, in answer to a disciple's question, Jesus teaches the lesson on forgiveness — the term seventy-seven itself being an illustration of mercy and forbearance. At Gamala, John's perplexity over "He who is not with me is against me" draws out the distinction between man's personal relation to the spiritual teachings of the kingdom and the outward social relations of believers over the work of other groups. Aden, the man whom John forbade to teach and work in Jesus' name, does not heed the injunction; he goes right on and raises up a considerable company of believers at Kanata. The mission of four weeks in the Decapolis, the paper concludes, was moderately successful.

Why it matters

Two moments carry this tour's challenge. The first is forgiveness reckoned at seventy-seven — mercy that refuses to keep a ledger of offenses, in a world where Lamech's exultation turned seventy-seven into a boast over superior weapons. The second is Aden: a man led to believe through the testimony of another, forbidden by an apostle to teach in the Master's name, who goes right on with his efforts and gathers believers anyway. The paper lets both stand. Institutional permission and actual fruit do not always travel together, and this tour honors the fruit — while Jesus' own teaching at Edrei keeps the workers themselves guided by the principles of preaching truth.
