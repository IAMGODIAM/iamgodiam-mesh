What this paper teaches

The paper first warns that many sayings and events of this night are easily misunderstood, then follows Judas from the Last Supper to his cousin's house and on to the captain of the temple guards, who gave him more than sixty guards, soldiers, and servants. It describes the armed band with torches and lanterns swinging around the hill while Judas stepped forward to identify Jesus with the traitorous kiss, the two groups standing motionless with the Master between them. Afterward the apostles and campers gathered at the olive press to deliberate, and the Jewish and Roman captains disputed where the prisoner should be taken.

Why it matters

This is an arrest staged as overwhelming force: torches, weapons, and sixty men sent at midnight against an unarmed teacher who had just refused to wake his own followers for a fight. The machinery of temple and empire works together, and its chosen instrument is an insider's kiss—intimacy converted into identification for the state. Communities that have lived under surveillance recognize the pattern. Yet the paper's center is not Judas but Jesus, sitting alone and unafraid on the olive press, declining both panic and retaliation. His calm is not surrender of the cause; it is mastery of the hour.
