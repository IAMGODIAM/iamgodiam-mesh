What did Jesus promise his apostles in the farewell discourse?

IAMGODIAM Knowledge Mesh · Urantia Book Study Guides · Paper 180 — The Farewell Discourse · Part IV — The Life and Teachings of Jesus · reviewed 2026-10-16

After the Psalm that closed the supper, the apostles expected to return to camp, but Jesus told them to sit down — there was a farewell to give. In this discourse he gave them a new commandment: to love one another even as he had loved them. He pictured himself as the true vine and his Father as the husbandman, urged them to abide in his love, answered their questions, and promised that after he went to the Father another helper would come — the Spirit of Truth, poured out upon all flesh.

“You have not merely chosen me, but I have also chosen you, and I have ordained you to go forth into the world to yield the fruit of loving service to your fellows even as I have lived among you and revealed the Father to you. The Father and I will both work with you, and you shall experience the divine fullness of joy if you will only obey my command to love one another, even as I have loved you.”

What this paper teaches

The discourse unfolds in movements. It opens realistically — troublous times are coming, and the apostles must now carry purse and provision rather than depend on the multitudes' good will. Then comes the new commandment, presented not as a burden but as joy: they are chosen and ordained to yield the fruit of loving service, as he has loved them. The vine-and-branches teaching follows — abide in his love by doing as he taught. After questions from Peter, James, John, and Matthew, he promises the new helper, the Spirit of Truth to be sent into believers' hearts, and says he tells them all this beforehand so they may be prepared.

Why it matters

The mark of this community is not a creed, a uniform, or a rank — it is love measured by his own: “even as I have loved you.” That standard dignifies every member, because the qualification for leadership here is fruit — loving service yielded in the world. And the promised helper is not reserved for the few in the room: the Spirit of Truth is to be poured out upon all flesh, into the hearts of believers everywhere. Farewell, in this paper, is not abandonment. It is a transfer — of love, of joy, and of presence — from one man to a whole people.
