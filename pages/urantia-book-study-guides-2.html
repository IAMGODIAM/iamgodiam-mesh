What is the nature of God?

IAMGODIAM Knowledge Mesh · Urantia Book Study Guides · Paper 2 — The Nature of God · Part I — The Central and Superuniverses · reviewed 2026-10-16

Paper 2 studies the character of Deity itself — what God is like when you look past names and titles to nature. Its portrait is of a Father who is infinite and eternal, without past, present, or future, for whom no cosmic event ever comes as a surprise. Yet this infinite God is righteous and therefore just, his mercy is justice tempered by perfect knowledge of creature weakness, and above every attribute stands the declaration that “God is love” — his only personal attitude toward the universe is divine affection.

“No thing is new to God, and no cosmic event ever comes as a surprise; he inhabits the circle of eternity. He is without beginning or end of days. To God there is no past, present, or future; all time is present at any given moment. He is the great and only I AM.”

“In these ways and in many others, in ways unknown to you and utterly beyond finite comprehension, does the Paradise Father lovingly and willingly downstep and otherwise modify, dilute, and attenuate his infinity in order that he may be able to draw nearer the finite minds of his creature children. And so, through a series of personality distributions which are diminishingly absolute, the infinite Father is enabled to enjoy close contact with the diverse intelligences of the many realms of his far-flung universe.”

Abba Talk corpus · 2:1.8 · read the passage

What this paper teaches

The paper is organized as a walk through the divine character. It begins with God's infinity — “Touching the Infinite, we cannot find him out” — and his eternity, the never-beginning, never-ending, circular nature the old prophets already understood. From there it takes up his righteousness and justice, then his mercy: justice tempered by the wisdom that comes from perfect knowledge of finite weakness. The center of the paper is its fifth section, on the love of God, before it turns to goodness, truth, and beauty — noting that God's goodness is found in the spiritual world of personal religious experience — and closes with the relativity of all finite knowledge.

Why it matters

Read this paper beside the experience of people judged by systems that never ask about circumstance. Its God does the opposite: his mercy is “justice tempered” by the full recognition of the natural weaknesses and environmental handicaps of finite creatures — judgment that begins by understanding the conditions a life was lived under. And the infinite Father does not wait at an unreachable distance. He willingly downsteps and dilutes his infinity, the paper says, precisely so he can draw nearer the finite minds of his creature children. Nearness, here, is not earned by the lowly. It is the deliberate movement of the high.
