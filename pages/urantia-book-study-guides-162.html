What happened when Jesus taught at the feast of tabernacles?

IAMGODIAM Knowledge Mesh · Urantia Book Study Guides · Paper 162 — At the Feast of Tabernacles · Part IV — The Life and Teachings of Jesus · reviewed 2026-10-16

Paper 162 follows Jesus up to Jerusalem for the feast of tabernacles. On the road, Samaritan villagers refuse the company lodging because they are Jews. In the city Jesus teaches boldly in the temple, proclaiming the liberty of the new gospel to pilgrims gathered from Spain to India. He deals with a woman of ill repute brought in by her accusers, speaks amid the feast's lights and processions, refuses his apostles' pleas to flee, and afterward instructs the believers gathered at Bethany.

“During the feast of tabernacles Jesus went boldly into Jerusalem on several occasions and publicly taught in the temple. This he did in spite of the efforts of his apostles to dissuade him. Though they had long urged him to proclaim his message in Jerusalem, they now feared to see him enter the city at this time, knowing full well that the scribes and Pharisees were bent on bringing about his death.”

“Jesus’ bold appearance in Jerusalem more than ever confused his followers. Many of his disciples, and even Judas Iscariot, the apostle, had dared to think that Jesus had fled in haste into Phoenicia because he feared the Jewish leaders and Herod Antipas. They failed to comprehend the significance of the Master’s movements. His presence in Jerusalem at the feast of tabernacles, even in opposition to the advice of his followers, sufficed forever to put an end to all whisperings about fear and cowardice.”

What this paper teaches

The followers had long urged Jesus to preach in Jerusalem so his message would carry the prestige of the capital; now they fear his boldness, knowing the scribes and Pharisees seek his death. The paper follows his temple teaching across the feast: the liberty of the new gospel and the joy of those who believe it, the woman brought before him by accusers and enemies, and his discourses on the feast's last days, including the great final day with its procession from the pool of Siloam. Precautions govern the nights — Jesus lodges at a friend's house with Lazarus and his sisters while the apostles scatter in small groups — and in the week after the feast, scores of believers receive instruction from the twelve at Bethany.

Why it matters

Jesus walks straight into the city whose rulers are plotting his death and speaks in the open, while still taking prudent precautions for the night. The paper holds courage and wisdom together: boldness is not recklessness, and fear does not set the agenda. His enemies also bring him a woman of ill repute, using her shame as a weapon in their campaign against him. For communities that know both state hostility and public shaming as tools of control, this feast week shows a teacher who will neither hide nor let a vulnerable person be used as a prop in someone else's trap.
