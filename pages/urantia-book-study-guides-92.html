How has religion evolved through the ages?

Paper 92 surveys the long career of evolutionary religion — the faith humanity built for itself out of its own endowments long before any systematic revelation reached the planet. It traces religion's rise from fear and ghosts, follows its slow adjustment to changing societies, and distinguishes natural religion from revealed religion, whose truth is evolutionary but always progressive. The paper closes by reading the religions of the twentieth century as a snapshot of man's worship impulse still on the march.

What this paper teaches

The paper opens by naming the inner influences that grew evolutionary religion: the worship urge in animal consciousness, the wisdom that directs adoration into higher channels, and the Holy Spirit's ministry, later augmented by Thought Adjusters, seraphim, and the Spirit of Truth. It then walks the stages — from coercing spirits to cajoling them, from tribal fetishes to tribal gods — and insists that revelation, though progressive, never cancels this evolutionary groundwork. Along the way it considers how religion clings rigidly yet tardily adjusts, how its study reads the fossil strata of past ages, and why religion itself can never become a scientific fact.

Why it matters

This paper refuses to look down on the faith of ancestors. The religions our forebears built from fear, ghosts, and hope were not foolishness; they were the slow work of real spiritual endowments growing in human minds. That matters for a people whose inherited faith is often mocked as primitive or merely emotional. The paper also holds a distinction worth keeping: revelation builds on what a people already know of God rather than erasing it — so the songs, prayers, and testimonies carried through suffering are not obstacles to truth but its living soil.
