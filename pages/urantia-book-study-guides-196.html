What was the faith of Jesus like?

IAMGODIAM Knowledge Mesh · Urantia Book Study Guides · Paper 196 — The Faith of Jesus · Part IV — The Life and Teachings of Jesus · reviewed 2026-10-16

Paper 196 closes the book by contemplating the faith Jesus himself lived. His trust in God’s watchcare was sublime and wholehearted, born of the divine presence within rather than inherited tradition or intellect alone. He saw God as holy, just, great, true, beautiful, and good, gathered into the will of the Father in heaven, and he invited followers not merely to believe in him but to believe with him as children of that Father.

“JESUS enjoyed a sublime and wholehearted faith in God. He experienced the ordinary ups and downs of mortal existence, but he never religiously doubted the certainty of God’s watchcare and guidance. His faith was the outgrowth of the insight born of the activity of the divine presence, his indwelling Adjuster. His faith was neither traditional nor merely intellectual; it was wholly personal and purely spiritual.”

“Jesus does not require his disciples to believe in him but rather to believe with him, believe in the reality of the love of God and in full confidence accept the security of the assurance of sonship with the heavenly Father. The Master desires that all his followers should fully share his transcendent faith. Jesus most touchingly challenged his followers, not only to believe what he believed, but also to believe as he believed. This is the full significance of his one supreme requirement, “Follow me.””

What this paper teaches

The paper first roots faith in experience: Jesus never religiously doubted God’s guidance through ordinary ups and downs, because his faith grew from the activity of his indwelling Adjuster and was wholly personal and purely spiritual. It then describes a higher type of religion based on personal spiritual relations with the Universal Father and validated by genuine personal experience, noting that strong faith in Jesus did not swell into fanaticism or religious ego. His devotion is presented as wholehearted consecration to the Father’s will and the service of man. Later the paper hopes a reformation may return to Jesus’ unadulterated teachings, and it calls personal spiritual experience an efficient solvent and adjuster of human problems.

Why it matters

The closing paper gives faith back to ordinary believers. If every mortal is a child of the Father of love, then no gatekeeper stands between a person and the trust Jesus modeled; disciples are asked to share his faith, not admire it from below. His believing was trusting like a child’s, yet free of presumption, and it held through disappointments, difficulties, and the stern requirements of duty. For people whose endurance is often demanded but rarely honored, this ending names steadfast faith itself as sacred work. Belief becomes a way of living, not a badge of belonging.
