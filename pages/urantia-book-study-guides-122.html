Why were Joseph and Mary chosen as Jesus' parents?

Paper 122 opens the birth narrative itself. Palestine was chosen as the land of Michael's bestowal, and from three couples nominated for the honor, Gabriel personally chose Joseph and Mary as the earth family of the child. The paper traces Joseph's ancestry, tells how Jesus' lifework was really begun by John the Baptist, and follows Gabriel's appearances, the journey to Bethlehem for the census, and the night of Jesus' birth.

“Jesus’ lifework on Urantia was really begun by John the Baptist. Zacharias, John’s father, belonged to the Jewish priesthood, while his mother, Elizabeth, was a member of the more prosperous branch of the same large family group to which Mary the mother of Jesus also belonged. Zacharias and Elizabeth, though they had been married many years, were childless.”

What this paper teaches

The paper moves through the choosing and the birth. It sketches Joseph's lineage, a Hebrew of the Hebrews carrying many non-Jewish strains added through the female lines of his ancestry. It tells of Zacharias and Elizabeth, long childless, and the son promised to them. It recounts Gabriel's appearance to Mary by a low stone table, and Joseph's impressive dream, which reconciled him to becoming father of an extraordinary child. It portrays Joseph as mild-mannered and extremely conscientious, describes the Nazareth home, the March journey in 8 B.C., the restless night of the birth, the temple redemption of the first-born, and Herod's watchers closing in.

Why it matters

Heaven's choice fell on a working household — a carpenter's family in a hill village, of mixed ancestry, faithful but far from powerful. The paper honors both parents: Mary personally selected and told the glad tidings, Joseph conscientious in every way toward his people's practices. And it does not hide the danger: a violent king's watchers were already moving against the child. For families who shelter hope under threat, and for ordinary people whose quiet faithfulness never makes headlines, this story insists that such a home is exactly where God entrusts the world's future.
