How does divine forgiveness work?

IAMGODIAM Knowledge Mesh · The Justice Reader · reviewed 2026-09-19

Forgiveness flows from the nature of God as an undivided, affectionate Father whose spirit indwells his children — not from a transaction or a technicality. Because God is not a divided personality, there is no part of him that must be persuaded to forgive; forgiveness is what love does when it meets repentance.

“The affectionate heavenly Father, whose spirit indwells his children on earth, is not a divided personality.”

Abba Talk corpus · 2:6.6 · read the passage

Not a transaction

Much of religious history has treated forgiveness as a deal: a price paid, a ledger balanced, a divine anger bought off. This passage dismantles the machinery. God is “not a divided personality” — there is no stern half that must be talked down by a merciful half. The Father’s spirit already indwells the child; forgiveness is not God changing his mind about you, it is you coming home to a love that never left. That reframes repentance entirely: not groveling before an offended monarch, but waking up inside a relationship that was already there.

What forgiveness asks of the forgiven

If forgiveness is the nature of the Father, then the children are called to the same nature — and here the teaching gets demanding. Communities that have been wronged are not asked to pretend the wrong didn’t happen; they are shown a God who sees the full truth of what was done and forgives anyway, from undivided love. That is a harder standard than vengeance and a harder standard than forgetting. It asks the wounded to become the kind of people in whom love is stronger than injury — not as a favor to the injurer, but as fidelity to their own divine parentage.
