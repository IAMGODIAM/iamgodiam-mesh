What this paper teaches

The paper first settles its method: time is reckoned in the present leap-year calendar of 365¼ days, exact years are of record but nearest whole numbers serve better, and dates run backward from the early twentieth century. Nebulae begin circular, grow spiral, and pass through stages of sun dispersion. Five billion years ago the sun stood as a comparatively isolated blazing orb, having gathered most of the near-by matter of space; after the solar system's birth came a period of diminishing solar disgorgement. The early space regions swarmed with small disruptive bodies that crashed directly into the forming worlds, and one billion years ago marks the actual beginning of Urantia history.

Why it matters

This paper gives our planet a birth certificate kept in archives older than humanity. Urantia is not cosmic debris; it is a registered world with antecedents of record, born of a nebula whose organization was authorized and overseen. For peoples whose histories were deliberately burned, renamed, and scattered, there is weight in a cosmos where every world's record is kept and none is anonymous. The paper also teaches scale: a billion years of history precede us. Our sufferings and triumphs are real, but they unfold inside a patience far older than empire — and the record-keepers have not lost our page.
